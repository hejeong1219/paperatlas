# LINE-1 Activation in Endocrine-Related Cancers: Molecular Mechanisms and Translational Implications

_Molecular Cancer Research, 2026._

## Summary

This review (Mol Cancer Res 2026;24:434–43) synthesizes evidence that long interspersed nuclear element-1 (LINE-1 / L1) retrotransposons — which comprise ~17% of the human genome and are normally epigenetically silenced in somatic cells — are pervasively derepressed across endocrine-related cancers (thyroid, adrenal, pituitary, prostate, ovary, breast, pancreas, neuroendocrine, and testicular germ-cell tumors). The authors organize the literature around three mechanistic consequences of L1 reactivation: (1) genomic instability via insertional mutagenesis, DNA double-strand breaks, replication stress, and copy-number alterations, with loss-of-function TP53/p53 acting as a key brake whose disruption permits ongoing retrotransposition; (2) epigenetic deregulation, with LINE-1 promoter hypomethylation used as a surrogate of global DNA demethylation and a permissive environment for L1 transcription and ORF1p/ORF2p expression; and (3) transcriptional/hormonal rewiring, most notably the emerging idea that ORF1p acts as a co-regulator of nuclear hormone receptors — in prostate cancer ORF1p directly interacts with the androgen receptor (AR), enhances AR transcriptional activity and PSA expression, and facilitates AR nuclear translocation. The review highlights translational angles: ORF1p (in tissue, plasma, ascites) and LINE-1 cfDNA metrics as surrogate biomarkers of epigenetic instability and early detection, and therapeutic targeting of the retrotransposon life cycle with reverse-transcriptase inhibitors (RTIs such as nevirapine, efavirenz, abacavir) and the neddylation inhibitor MLN4924 (pevonedistat). The single P-body-relevant assertion is a cited (not new) result: in prostate cancer, RNA immunoprecipitation showed ORF1p binds both LINE-1 and non-LINE-1 RNAs, including polyadenylated transcripts and those enriched in P-bodies (Briggs et al. 2021, ref. 13). This is a review with no new primary experimental data; nearly all relations are background/annotation rather than mechanistic perturbations.

## Key Points

- **LINE-1 biology and life cycle**: L1 is an autonomous retrotransposon (~17% of the genome) encoding ORF1p (an RNA-binding protein with nucleic-acid chaperone activity) and ORF2p (endonuclease + reverse transcriptase). The bicistronic mRNA is translated, ORF1p/ORF2p bind their parent mRNA to form a ribonucleoprotein (RNP), and after nuclear reimport L1 integrates via target-primed reverse transcription.
- **Derepression is a shared hallmark of endocrine tumors**: LINE-1 hypomethylation is the most consistently reported alteration, associated with advanced stage, metastasis, and poor prognosis (notably prostate, ovarian, pancreatic). ORF1p expression links to AR signaling in prostate cancer and to platinum resistance in ovarian cancer. Dysregulation extends even to benign tumors (aldosteronoma, pituitary).
- **p53/TP53 is a brake on retrotransposition**: induced L1 expression causes double-strand breaks and replication stress; L1 expression correlates with p53-mutation status and copy-number alterations; loss-of-function TP53 strongly associates with increased somatic L1 insertions in pancreatic tumors, traceable across clonal phylogenies.
- **ORF1p as a hormone-receptor co-regulator**: in prostate cancer cells ORF1p enhanced AR transcriptional activity and PSA expression and directly interacted with AR to facilitate nuclear translocation and recruitment to androgen-responsive promoters — proposed as an AR co-regulator supporting proliferation and therapy resistance.
- **P-body connection (cited)**: in prostate cancer, RIP-seq / L1EM analysis revealed ORF1p binds LINE-1 and non-LINE-1 RNAs, including polyadenylated transcripts and those enriched in P-bodies; ORF1p-bound RNAs were upregulated in LINE-1-expressing prostate tumors (TCGA), suggesting a role in posttranscriptional regulation.
- **Biomarkers**: circulating anti-ORF1p/ORF2p autoantibodies (IgG) elevated early in lung, pancreatic, ovarian, esophageal, liver cancers; ultrasensitive Simoa plasma ORF1p detection (mid-attomolar) with high specificity (98% in ovarian); LINE-1 cfDNA integrity reduced in advanced neuroendocrine neoplasia, correlating with tumor burden.
- **Therapeutics**: RTIs (nevirapine, efavirenz, abacavir) impair proliferation and induce redifferentiation in thyroid and prostate models (e.g., nevirapine restores NIS/TSHR and radioiodine uptake); neddylation inhibitor MLN4924 (pevonedistat) suppresses ORF2p function and retrotransposition via UBE2M-dependent Cullin-RING ligase activation. Most evidence is preclinical/anecdotal; RTIs are pleiotropic, so retrotransposition-blockade specificity in vivo is unproven.
- **Detection methods**: methylation (COBRA, LUMA, pyrosequencing, WGBS), insertion callers (MELT, PALMER, SCRAMble, xTea), and expression quantifiers (L1EM, TEtranscripts, SQuIRE) on bulk/single-cell RNA-seq; ~150 retrotransposition-competent L1 loci exist but only a small "hot" subset drives most somatic insertions.

## Scope

Covers: the L1 retrotransposition life cycle (ORF1p/ORF2p, RNP intermediate, target-primed reverse transcription); the spectrum of LINE-1–associated molecular events across endocrine-related tumors (hypomethylation, ORF1p/ORF2p expression, somatic insertions, 3'-transduction); mechanistic consequences (genomic instability, replication stress, p53/TP53 disruption, hormone-receptor co-regulation via ORF1p–AR); translational applications (ORF1p tissue/plasma/cfDNA biomarkers, RTI and neddylation-inhibitor therapeutics); and bioinformatic/experimental approaches to detect somatic L1 insertions. As a review it presents no new primary data and summarizes correlative, largely cross-sectional studies; the authors repeatedly note that causality (driver vs. passenger), retrotransposition vs. hypomethylation, and tumor-specificity remain unresolved. The single direct intersection with P-body biology is a cited finding (ORF1p binds P-body-enriched mRNAs in prostate cancer); P-body assembly, decapping/decay machinery, and condensate biophysics are not addressed.

## Open Questions

- Does the higher ORF1p expression observed in pituitary and ovarian tumors translate into increased retrotransposition or altered transcriptional regulation of nearby genes, or is it a passive marker of epigenetic relaxation?
- What is the functional consequence of ORF1p binding P-body-enriched, polyadenylated mRNAs in prostate cancer — does ORF1p partition into or sequester transcripts within P-bodies, and how does this relate to its AR co-regulatory role?
- Is LINE-1 hypomethylation an early driver event or a secondary consequence of dedifferentiation in endocrine tumors (most studies are cross-sectional)?
- Through what mechanism does ORF1p function as an AR co-regulator at androgen-responsive promoters, and is this generalizable to other nuclear hormone receptors?
- Can the retrotransposition-blockade contribution of RTIs be separated from their pleiotropic effects in vivo, and do LINE-1–activated endocrine tumors define a responder subgroup?
