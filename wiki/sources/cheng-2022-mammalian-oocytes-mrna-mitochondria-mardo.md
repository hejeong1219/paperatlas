# Mammalian oocytes store mRNAs in a mitochondria-associated membraneless compartment

_Science, 2022._

## Summary

Mammalian oocytes cease transcription during the final stages of growth and rely on stored maternal mRNAs to make proteins through meiotic maturation and early embryogenesis, but where and how those mRNAs are stored had remained unknown. Through a localization screen of highly expressed RNA-binding proteins in full-grown mouse oocytes, the authors found that ZAR1, YBX2, DDX6, LSM14B, and 4E-T (EIF4ENIF1) co-localize with mitochondria, forming clusters throughout the cytoplasm, and that poly(A) mRNAs are stored in this same domain (RNA-FISH). Because this domain was distinct from any previously known RNA-containing compartment, they named it the **mitochondria-associated ribonucleoprotein domain (MARDO)**. The MARDO is conserved across mammalian species, including human, bovine, and porcine oocytes. Its assembly is directed by the rise in mitochondrial membrane potential during oocyte growth, and the RNA-binding protein ZAR1 — through its disordered N-terminal domain, which phase-separates into hydrogel-like condensates — drives MARDO coalescence and clustering of mitochondria. mRNAs stored in the MARDO are translationally repressed; loss of ZAR1 disrupts the MARDO, disperses mitochondria, and causes premature loss of MARDO-localized mRNAs. During the meiosis I-to-meiosis II transition, proteasomal degradation of ZAR1 dissolves the MARDO, which is required for the timely degradation of maternal mRNAs. The work thus reveals a physical and functional coupling between a membraneless compartment (MARDO) and a membrane-bound organelle (mitochondria) that coordinates maternal mRNA storage, translation, and decay to ensure fertility in mammals.

## Key Points

- **MARDO components.** RNA-binding proteins ZAR1, YBX2, DDX6, LSM14B, and 4E-T (EIF4ENIF1) co-localize with mitochondria (cytochrome c) in mouse GV oocytes, forming clusters throughout the cytoplasm; high-resolution (Airyscan) imaging shows ZAR1 accumulating around individual mitochondria. ZAR1 also co-localizes with the poly(A)-binding protein PABPC1L and with the other four RNA-binding proteins.
- **mRNAs are stored there.** RNA-FISH with Cy5-oligo d(T)30 shows poly(A)-tailed mRNAs co-localizing with ZAR1 around mitochondria; single-molecule RNA-FISH confirms that specific maternal transcripts (e.g. Tcstv1, Wdr37, Elovl7) are enriched in the MARDO, unlike the control mRNA Actb.
- **Naming and specificity.** Because the domain is distinct from any known RNA-containing compartment, the authors named it the mitochondria-associated ribonucleoprotein domain (MARDO). ZAR1 did **not** associate with the Golgi apparatus (GM130), recycling endosomes (RAB11A), or lysosomes (LAMP1), and associated with only a fraction of ER tubules (BCAP31); a proximity ligation assay (PLA) confirmed the specific ZAR1-mitochondria (TOMM20) association over a recycling-endosome marker.
- **Conservation.** ZAR1 and other RNA-binding proteins also accumulate around mitochondria, forming cytoplasmic clusters, in human, porcine, and bovine oocytes — establishing the MARDO across mammalian species.
- **Assembly directed by mitochondrial membrane potential.** The MARDO gradually appears as oocytes grow and is most prominent in full-grown "surrounded nucleolus" (SN) oocytes, whose mitochondria are most active. Mitochondrial membrane potential (TMRM/MitoTracker Green ratio) increases during growth and peaks in SN oocytes; depolarizing mitochondria with antimycin A, FCCP, or oligomycin A disrupts the MARDO, showing the membrane-potential increase is required for assembly.
- **ZAR1 drives coalescence and mitochondrial clustering.** Among the MARDO components, overexpressed ZAR1 (followed by LSM14B) was most enriched on mitochondria and uniquely drove coalescence of MARDO foci and mitochondria into giant clusters (confirmed by EM and immuno-EM); other RNA-binding proteins or mScarlet alone did not. The coalesced MARDO contained mitochondria but largely excluded Golgi, endosomes, lysosomes, ribosomes, and most ER tubules.
- **Phase separation / hydrogel properties.** MARDO behavior is consistent with phase separation: partial photobleaching shows internal rearrangement, but coalescence and FRAP recovery are slow (hydrogel-like, not liquid-like); 1,6-hexanediol destroys the MARDO; RNase injection promotes coalescence/clustering within ~4 min. The disordered N-terminal domain of ZAR1 [ZAR1(1-263)] is necessary and sufficient for coalescence and phase-separates in vitro into slow-recovering hydrogel-like condensates (whereas a solubilizing SUMO/SMT3 tag yields liquid-like droplets).
- **ZAR1 is essential.** Zar1 knockout (and RNAi or Trim-Away depletion) disrupts the MARDO and disperses mitochondria — most strikingly at MI, where MARDO and mitochondria normally cluster around the spindle. mRNA no longer accumulates around mitochondria in knockouts; phenotypes are rescued by re-expressing full-length ZAR1 (N-terminal domain restores clustering; full-length is more efficient at concentrating YBX2 via the C-terminal RNA-binding domain). Depleting DDX6 by Trim-Away does **not** affect MARDO formation.
- **Translational repression / storage.** RNA-seq of Zar1-knockout oocytes shows >1000 mRNAs reduced by ≥50%, and an increase in translation- and mitochondria-related transcripts. Translating ribosomes (RPL24/phospho-RPS6 PLA) and polysomes (EM) are largely excluded from the MARDO; tethering ZAR1 (or LSM14B) to a reporter mRNA (PP7/tdPCP) represses its translation — indicating MARDO-stored mRNAs are translationally repressed.
- **Dissolution couples to mRNA decay.** ZAR1 is phosphorylated by CDK1 (sites T154, S161) when meiosis resumes, but phosphorylation does not regulate MARDO dynamics; instead, proteasomal degradation of ZAR1 (blocked by MG-132, independent of cell-cycle progression as shown with nocodazole) drives MARDO dissolution during the MI-to-MII transition. Delaying dissolution by ZAR1 overexpression retains MARDO-localized mRNAs in MII oocytes and impairs their degradation, showing MARDO dissolution is required for timely maternal mRNA decay (~79% of maternal mRNAs are normally degraded during maturation).
- **Functional consequences.** Loss of ZAR1 causes premature loss of mRNAs known to be translationally activated later, and live imaging shows severe defects in spindle assembly, chromosome alignment, and cytokinesis during meiotic maturation, consistent with female infertility in Zar1-knockout mice.

## Methods

- **Localization screen / immunofluorescence** in full-grown mouse GV oocytes for RNA-binding proteins (ZAR1, YBX2, DDX6, LSM14B, 4E-T, PABPC1L) vs. organelle markers (mitochondria cytochrome c/COX17/TOMM20/FIS1; ER BCAP31/KDEL; Golgi GM130; recycling endosomes RAB11A; lysosomes LAMP1); confocal and Airyscan microscopy.
- **RNA-FISH** with Cy5-oligo d(T)30 for poly(A) mRNAs and **smRNA-FISH** (ViewRNA) for specific transcripts (Tcstv1, Wdr37, Elovl7; Actb control).
- **Proximity ligation assay (PLA)** for ZAR1-TOMM20, ZAR1-RAB11A, DDX6-TOMM20, and RPL24/phospho-RPS6 (translating ribosomes).
- **Mitochondrial membrane potential** measured by TMRM/MitoTracker Green ratio; perturbed with antimycin A, FCCP, oligomycin A; Mito-EGFP labeling.
- **Live-cell imaging / FRAP** of mScarlet- and mClover3-tagged ZAR1 and other components; 1,6-hexanediol and RNase injection assays; mitochondrial clustering index quantification.
- **ZAR1 truncations** [N-terminal ZAR1(1-263); C-terminal ZAR1(252-361)] expressed in oocytes; **in vitro phase separation / FRAP** with purified SMT3-ZAR1(1-263) and tag-cleaved ZAR1(1-263).
- **Genetic / depletion tools:** CRISPR/Cas9 Zar1-knockout mice; RNAi (siRNA) and Trim-Away (TRIM21) protein depletion of ZAR1 and DDX6; rescue by re-expression.
- **Omics:** RNA-seq of Zar1-knockout vs. wild-type oocytes; RT-qPCR validation; mass-spectrometry phosphoproteomics of GV vs. MI oocytes; in vitro CDK1 phosphorylation assay (T154/S161).
- **Translation reporter** (mClover3-2×PP7 + mScarlet control, tethered via tdPCP-ZAR1 or tdPCP-LSM14B).
- **Electron microscopy / immunogold** of mitochondrial clusters, polysomes, and ZAR1 localization. Oocytes from mouse, plus human, bovine, and porcine oocytes (human immature oocytes from assisted-reproduction patients under ethics approval).

## Connections

- [P-Bodies and mRNA Regulation](../topics/p-bodies-and-mrna-regulation.md) — the MARDO shares components with P-bodies: the DDX6-LSM14B-4E-T complex is present in both, and 4E-T/LSM14B are canonical P-body proteins. The authors note the MARDO is distinct from P-bodies, stress granules, P granules, and polar granules because it co-localizes with mitochondria, whereas those compartments do not (or only partially) do.
- [Li 2023 — LSM14B in oocyte RNA-binding and maternal mRNA metabolism](../sources/li-2023-lsm14b-oocyte-rna-binding-maternal-mrna-metabolism.md) — LSM14B is a MARDO component and the second-most-enriched protein on mitochondria after ZAR1; both papers concern maternal mRNA storage/metabolism in oocytes.
- [Hubstenberger 2017 — P-body purification reveals condensation of repressed mRNA regulons](../sources/hubstenberger-2017-pbody-purification-condensation-repressed-mrna-regulons.md) — shared DDX6/LSM14/4E-T translational-repression machinery; both describe condensates that store translationally repressed mRNAs while excluding ribosomes/polysomes, but the MARDO is mitochondria-associated and hydrogel-like.

## Open Questions

- What is the function of the clustered mitochondria, and does concentrating active mitochondria in high-energy-demand regions (e.g. around the spindle) minimize ROS production?
- Does the DDX6-LSM14B-4E-T complex perform the same translational-regulation functions in the MARDO as it does in P-bodies and stress granules?
- Does MARDO dissolution require FAF2-VCP (p97)-mediated extraction of ubiquitinated ZAR1, by analogy to G3BP1 in stress-granule disassembly (Zar1 interacts with VCP in Xenopus)?
- Is a MARDO present in Xenopus oocytes, and what is the role of ZAR1 in its assembly relative to the Balbiani body (which forms earlier, clusters mitochondria via an amyloid-like rather than hydrogel matrix, and contains Golgi)?
- How exactly does the C-terminal RNA-binding domain of ZAR1 select and recruit specific maternal mRNAs to the MARDO, and how is their later translational activation timed?
