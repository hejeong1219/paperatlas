# Inhibition of long interspersed nuclear element-1 by nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors attenuates vascular calcification

_Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy, 2025._

## Summary

Vascular calcification (VC) — the osteogenic transdifferentiation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) into bone-like cells — is a major driver of cardiovascular morbidity in chronic kidney disease (CKD), yet lacks effective therapy. This study identifies the retrotransposon **long interspersed nuclear element-1 (LINE1/LINE-1)** as a driver of VC and **nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs)**, validated LINE1 inhibitors, as a candidate repurposed therapy. LINE1 (its ORF1p protein, mRNA and cytoplasmic DNA) is significantly upregulated in calcified arteries of CKD patients and in two VC mouse models (high-adenine-diet CKD and vitamin D3 overload), and in Pi-induced calcified human VSMCs, where it tracks with osteogenic markers (Runx2, OPN) and inversely with contractile markers (SM22α, Smoothelin). Silencing LINE1 (siRNA) or inhibiting its reverse transcriptase activity (NRTIs lamivudine and entecavir) blocked osteogenic reprogramming of VSMCs and attenuated VC in vivo. Mechanistically, bulk RNA-seq showed LINE1 inhibition (si-LINE1 or NRTIs) downregulates the **cGAS–STING** signaling pathway and downstream inflammatory genes; the program is dominated by immune/viral-infection and cytokine–cytokine-receptor pathways. Functional epistasis placed cGAS–STING downstream of LINE1: STING activation (cGAMP) exacerbated VC while STING inhibition (H-151) or cGAS knockdown rescued it, and cGAS knockdown / H-151 reversed the VC-aggravating effect of LINE1 overexpression. ChIP-qPCR demonstrated that **cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA directly binds cGAS**, with binding elevated in calcified VSMCs, further increased by LINE1 overexpression, and reduced by NRTIs — identifying LINE1-derived cDNA as a direct cGAS activator. A cross-sectional clinical study of 1,785 hepatitis-B/HIV patients found NRTI users had lower VC prevalence (25.2% vs 35.4%, P < 0.001), with multivariate logistic regression establishing NRTI use as an independent protective factor against VC.

## Key Points

- **LINE1 is upregulated in human and mouse VC.** LINE1 mRNA in PBMCs was higher in CKD patients with VC vs without (1.61 vs 4.63, P < 0.001; n = 25 vs 12) and positively correlated with the VC Agatston score (P < 0.001); IHC/IF showed high LINE1-ORF1 with high Runx2 in calcified radial arteries.
- **LINE1 DNA, mRNA and ORF1p rise in calcified VSMCs and mouse aortas.** In Pi-induced calcified VSMCs, LINE1 DNA and mRNA increased in both cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions; LINE1-ORF1 rose dose-dependently with Pi alongside OPN/Runx2 up and SM22α/Smoothelin down; ORF1p was elevated in calcified aortas of CKD and VitD3-overloaded mice.
- **Silencing LINE1 blocks VSMC osteogenic reprogramming.** si-LINE1 reduced calcium deposits (Alizarin red, calcium content), suppressed osteogenic markers (Runx2, OPN) and restored contractile markers (SM22α, Smoothelin).
- **NRTIs (lamivudine; entecavir) inhibit LINE1 and VSMC calcification dose-dependently.** NRTIs lowered LINE1 DNA/mRNA and ORF1p in VSMCs and produced a dose-dependent (1, 5, 10, 50 μM) reduction in calcium deposition and osteogenic markers; entecavir reproduced the effect.
- **NRTIs attenuate VC in two in vivo models.** Daily NRTI injection (1 mg/kg) reduced aortic calcification (whole-aorta Alizarin red, Von Kossa, calcium content) in CKD and VitD3 models, lowered ORF1p/OPN/Runx2 and raised SM22α/Smoothelin, and extended CKD-mouse survival — without changing renal function (creatinine, BUN) or renal fibrosis.
- **LINE1 inhibition downregulates the cGAS–STING pathway.** Bulk RNA-seq of Pi-VSMCs with si-LINE1 or NRTIs found 1,762 common DEGs enriched for immune/infectious-disease and cytokine–cytokine-receptor pathways; cGAS and STING and downstream inflammatory genes were significantly downregulated by both interventions.
- **cGAS–STING activation is required for VC.** cGAS-STING molecules rose Pi-dose-dependently in VSMCs; cGAMP (second messenger) increased in calcified VSMCs and CKD serum; the STING agonist cGAMP accelerated calcium deposition and osteogenic switching, whereas the STING antagonist H-151 reduced calcium deposits and reversed osteogenic change.
- **LINE1 acts upstream of cGAS–STING.** NRTIs and si-LINE1 suppressed cGAS, STING, p-TBK1 and p-IRF3 in calcified VSMCs and lowered inflammatory mediators (IFNA, IFNE, IL-1β, IL-6, NF-κB); LINE1 overexpression (Ad-LINE1) activated cGAS-STING, an effect blocked by NRTIs.
- **Cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA directly binds and activates cGAS.** ChIP-qPCR showed binding of cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA to cGAS was higher in calcified VSMCs, further enhanced by LINE1 overexpression and reduced by NRTIs — identifying LINE1-derived cDNA as a direct cGAS activator.
- **cGAS/STING blockade rescues LINE1-driven VC.** LINE1 overexpression exacerbated VC (Alizarin red, calcium content, osteogenic Western blot), and this was significantly reversed by cGAS knockdown (si-cGAS) or H-151, placing cGAS–STING epistatically downstream of LINE1.
- **NRTI use is independently protective in a 1,785-patient cohort.** NRTI users had lower VC prevalence (25.2% vs 35.4%, P < 0.001) and lower aortic-arch/coronary calcium scores; multivariate logistic regression gave OR 0.535 (95% CI 0.335–0.843, P = 0.007) in the fully adjusted Model 4. Protection held in elderly (OR 0.410) and CKD subgroups (OR 0.383) but not in diabetic patients.
- **Proposed Pi→LINE1 link via heterochromatin/SIRT6.** The authors discuss (from prior literature) that high Pi may activate LINE1 by inhibiting SIRT6-mediated maintenance of heterochromatin, since SIRT6 represses LINE1 and is itself suppressed under calcification conditions.

## Methods

- **Human cohorts**: 37 CKD patients (PBMC LINE1 mRNA, thoracic-aorta Agatston scoring by chest MDCT, blinded); CKD radial-artery specimens (6 calcified arteriovenous-fistula patients vs non-calcified amputation controls) for IHC/IF; cross-sectional study of 1,785 HIV/hepatitis-B inpatients (801 NRTI users, 984 non-users) with MDCT calcium scoring (>130 HU; Agatston >0 = VC positive) and univariate/multivariate logistic regression (Models 1–4, multiple imputation).
- **VSMC culture / calcification**: human primary VSMCs (ATCC), calcified with 3.0 mmol/L inorganic phosphate (Pi); LINE1 siRNA transfection (50 nM); drug treatments NRTI lamivudine (HY-B0250), entecavir, STING agonist cGAMP (HY-12512), STING antagonist H-151 (HY-112693); Ad-LINE1 overexpression; si-cGAS knockdown.
- **Mouse VC models**: C57BL/6J males; CKD-VC via high-adenine (0.15%)/phosphorus (1.9%) diet 12 weeks; VitD3-overload via VitD3 (500 IU/kg) i.p. ×3 days; NRTI (1 mg/kg) or vehicle daily i.p.
- **Readouts**: Alizarin red staining, Von Kossa staining, calcium content quantification, Western blot (Runx2, OPN, SM22α, Smoothelin, LINE1-ORF1, cGAS, STING, p-TBK1, p-IRF3), IHC/IF, qPCR (IFNA, IFNE, IL-1β, IL-6, NF-κB), ELISA (serum cytokines), cGAMP quantification, nuclear/cytoplasmic LINE1 DNA & mRNA fractionation.
- **Genomics**: bulk RNA-seq of Pi-VSMCs ± si-LINE1 / NRTIs (GEO GSE279148); DEG/Venn/GO/KEGG analysis.
- **Direct interaction**: ChIP-qPCR of cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA bound to cGAS under si-LINE1, Ad-LINE1, and NRTI conditions.

## Open Questions

- **Is the LINE1→cGAS–STING→VC axis causal in humans?** The clinical study is cross-sectional and cannot establish a causal LINE1–VC link or do dose-response; the authors call for longitudinal prospective NRTI trials.
- **Why does NRTI protection fail in diabetics?** Protection was absent in diabetic and CVD subgroups; the authors attribute this to AGE/endothelial-dysfunction-driven (vs phosphate-driven) calcification mechanisms, but this is untested here.
- **What is the exact Pi→LINE1 trigger?** A SIRT6/heterochromatin-loss mechanism is proposed from prior literature but not directly demonstrated in this study.
- **Does LINE1 ORF2p reverse transcriptase generate the cytoplasmic cDNA sensed by cGAS, and is that the NRTI target step?** NRTIs inhibit reverse transcription and reduce LINE1 cDNA–cGAS binding, implicating RT-generated cDNA, but the enzymatic source is inferred rather than dissected.
