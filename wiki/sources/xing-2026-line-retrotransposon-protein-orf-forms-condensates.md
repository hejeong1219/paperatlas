# LINE-1 Retrotransposon Protein ORF1p Forms Condensates That Drive cGAS-Induced Immune Evasion in Lung Squamous Cell Carcinoma

_Cancer Research, 2026._

## Summary

Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) is driven by an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and lacks actionable drivers. This study links aberrantly reactivated pathogenic LINE-1 retrotransposons (pL1HS) to immune evasion in LUSC through a **liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS)–driven cGAS-STING axis**. Integrated multiomics (bulk + single-cell RNA-seq, proteomics) of patient cohorts showed that pL1HS+ LUSC carries a suppressive, myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC)–rich, T-cell–exhausted microenvironment with hyperactivated cGAS-STING signaling and predicts worse survival. Mechanistically, the LINE-1 open reading frame 1 protein (**L1-ORF1p**) undergoes LLPS via its intrinsically disordered region (IDR), forming cytosolic condensates that scaffold pL1HS DNA:RNA hybrid nucleic acid, the chromatin protein **HMGN2**, and **cGAS** into a ternary complex. The condensate concentrates immunostimulatory pL1HS DNA to chronically activate cGAS-STING, which signals through a dominant noncanonical NF-κB arm (IL6) to recruit and activate CD33+CD11b+HLA-DR−/low (human) / CD11b+Gr-1+ (mouse) MDSCs and drive T-cell exhaustion and Treg accumulation. The C-terminal coiled-coil domain (CCD) of L1-ORF1p stabilizes the condensate by binding HMGN2, while the IDR drives the phase separation that concentrates the DNA trigger. Disrupting condensates pharmacologically (1,6-hexanediol), genetically (IDR/C-CCD truncations, HMGN2 knockdown), or inhibiting LINE-1 reverse transcriptase (nevirapine, NVR) or cGAS (RU.521) each collapses the cGAS-STING output and MDSC induction. In vivo, **dual inhibition of LINE-1 and cGAS** (and cGAS inhibitor + anti–PD-1) synergistically suppressed tumor growth and reversed the suppressive microenvironment in pL1HS+ LUSC mouse models, defining a therapeutically actionable LLPS-driven immune-evasion axis.

## Key Points

- **pL1HS is highly prevalent and prognostic in LUSC.** 83.9% of smoking-history TCGA-LUSC (n = 392) and 84.7% of TJMUCH-LUSC (n = 150) samples carried L1HS elements (highest prevalence vs L1PA2/L1PB1). Four L1HS insertions (L1-FGGY, L1-ATP8B1, L1-SVEP1, L1-PTEN), exclusively tumor-expressed at >10% frequency and prognostic, were defined as pathogenic pL1HS; pL1HS+ patients had significantly shorter overall survival (TJMUCH P = 0.011; TCGA early-stage I–II P = 0.023). Median pL1HS expression was far higher in smokers (median 257) than nonsmokers (median 5.5) in TCGA.
- **pL1HS+ LUSC has a suppressive microenvironment.** GO/KEGG of DEGs showed suppression of MHC II complex and lymphocyte-mediated immunity; GSVA showed attenuated TCR signaling, fewer tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, and impaired antigen processing/presentation in pL1HS+ vs pL1HS− tumors.
- **scRNA-seq: Tregs, exhausted CD8+ T cells, and MDSCs dominate pL1HS+ tumors.** CD3+ (CD4+/CD8+) T cells decreased; Tregs (FOXP3, TIGIT, CTLA4 high) and terminally exhausted CD8+ T cells (LAG3, HAVCR2/TIM3 high) increased; immunosuppressive IL1B+ and CCL4L2+ myeloid cells expanded. Spatial mIHC (Xinchao TMA, n = 90) confirmed elevated CD11b+ MDSC infiltration in pL1HS+ LUSC (quantified by L1-ORF1p as a proxy), inversely correlated with CD8+ T-cell abundance.
- **pL1HS activates cGAS-STING in tumor cells.** SK-MES-1OV-pL1HS upregulated IFNα/IFNγ responses (NES = 2.18 / 2.01) and IL6–JAK–STAT3 (NES = 2.05), cytosolic DNA-sensing (NES = 1.65), RIG-I (NES = 2.09) and Toll-like receptor (NES = 1.82) pathways; raised CGAS and IFNB1; increased p-STING, p-TBK1, p-IRF3, cytokines (IL1α, IL1β, IL6, IL8, CXCL10, IFNα/β/γ), cGAMP, and secreted IL6.
- **NF-κB (IL6), not type I IFN, drives MDSC induction.** In SK-MES-1OV-pL1HS + THP1 cocultures, neutralizing IL6 strongly reduced CD33+CD11b+HLA-DR−/low MDSC generation, whereas neutralizing CXCL10 (IFN-I) had only slight effect; NF-κB activation (pIκB, pIKKα/β) accompanied MDSC induction. NVR or cGAS inhibitor (RU.521, 10 μmol/L) reversed MDSC induction and NF-κB phosphorylation.
- **L1-ORF1p forms cytosolic condensates with LLPS behavior.** mIHC of 40 LUSC cases showed cytoplasmic L1-ORF1p condensates only in pL1HS+ tumors. FRAP in SK-MES-1OV-L1-ORF1p-mEGFP and H520 showed bidirectional molecular exchange with the dilute phase; purified L1-ORF1p-mEGFP formed discrete droplets at physiologic 200 nmol/L in vitro and recovered after photobleaching.
- **LLPS is required for cGAS-STING activation.** 1,6-hexanediol (1.5%, 5 min) dissolved L1-ORF1p condensates in SK-MES-1OV-pL1HS, diminished cGAS enzyme activity / cGAMP release, reduced STING/pTBK1/pIRF3, repressed cytokines (IL1α, IL1β, IL6, IL8, CXCL10, IFNα/β/γ), and curtailed MDSC induction.
- **Both the IDR and C-terminal CCD are needed.** L1-ORF1pΔIDR fully abolished condensates; L1-ORF1pΔC-CCD partially impaired them, while ΔN-CCD retained condensate capacity. ΔIDR and ΔC-CCD reduced p-STING/p-TBK1; ΔN-CCD had negligible effect.
- **pL1HS DNA:RNA hybrids are incorporated into the condensate.** EMSA and ChIP-qPCR confirmed L1-ORF1p binds pL1HS DNA (CCD-dependent); Cy5-labeled pL1HS DNA co-condensed with L1-ORF1p, peaking at a 1:10 molar ratio. RNase A reduced cytosolic pL1HS mRNA and slightly reduced condensate amount, supporting DNA:RNA-hybrid stabilization of condensates.
- **HMGN2 is a condensate partner that stabilizes the assembly.** IP/MS identified HMGN2 (upregulated in pL1HS+ cells) as an L1-ORF1p interactor; endogenous/exogenous co-IP and EMSA confirmed direct L1-ORF1p–HMGN2 binding (independent of pL1HS DNA). HMGN2 colocalized in condensates; shHMGN2 reduced condensates, inhibited cGAS-STING activation, lowered cGAMP/cytokines, and restrained MDSC induction. ITC showed an exothermic L1-ORF1p–HMGN2 interaction; AlphaFold3 modeled the L1-ORF1p–pL1HS DNA–HMGN2 complex.
- **Proposed tripartite mechanism.** C-terminal CCD-mediated HMGN2 binding stabilizes condensates while IDR-driven LLPS concentrates sufficient pL1HS DNA to trigger cGAS-STING; cGAS also binds pL1HS DNA, and L1-ORF1p–cGAS interaction is stronger in OV-pL1HS than OV-L1-ORF1p cells (bridged by pL1HS DNA).
- **Chronic noncanonical NF-κB underlies protumor output.** The chronic activation favors a noncanonical NF-κB / IL6 program (immune evasion) over acute IRF3/type-I-IFN antitumor immunity, consistent with simultaneous p-IRF3 and noncanonical NF-κB activation with IL6 as the dominant MDSC inducer.
- **Dual targeting reverses immune evasion in vivo.** In KLN205OV-L1Md BDF1 mice, pL1HS+ tumors were larger with elevated ORF1p/cGAS, ISGs (IFNα/β, CXCL10), NF-κB cytokines (IL1β, IL6, IL8), MDSCs (CD11b+Gr-1+, especially M-MDSCs), Tregs (CD25+FOXP3+) and exhausted (TIM3+PD-1+) CD8+ T cells. Combined NVR + RU.521 produced the greatest tumor shrinkage and MDSC depletion; cGAS inhibitor + anti–PD-1 synergized (anti–PD-1 alone was weak), reversing the suppressive microenvironment.

## Methods

- **Patient cohorts / bioinformatics**: TCGA-LUSC (n = 392, smokers), TJMUCH-LUSC (n = 150), GSE181043 RNA-seq (n = 92), and an external TMA validation cohort (Xinchao, n = 90). L1 insertion calling with survminer-optimized cutoffs; edgeR/limma DEGs; clusterProfiler GO/KEGG; GSVA immune signatures (ImmPort + 29 immune signatures).
- **Single-cell RNA-seq**: 10x Genomics on fresh/cryopreserved pL1HS+ tumor–normal pairs and pL1HS+ vs pL1HS− tumors; Seurat + Harmony, Louvain clustering, Monocle2 pseudotime.
- **Cell models**: SK-MES-1 and NCI-H520 LUSC lines overexpressing L1-ATP8B1 (OV-pL1HS) or L1-ORF1p (± mEGFP tag); mouse KLN205OV-L1Md (L1Md-A102 5′UTR + ORF1p); THP1- and PBMC-based MDSC induction; coculture/neutralization (anti-IL6, anti-CXCL10); CFSE T-cell suppression assays.
- **Mechanistic readouts**: immunofluorescence + FRAP (Zeiss LSM 880), in vitro LLPS of purified GST-tagged L1-ORF1p(-mEGFP) and truncations (ΔIDR, ΔN-CCD, ΔC-CCD) and GST-HMGN2-mCherry; EMSA (Cy5-pL1HS DNA probe, cold competition); ChIP-qPCR (cGAS, L1-ORF1p); co-IP/IP-MS; ITC; AlphaFold3 docking; RNase A; western blot; ELISA/cytometric bead array (cGAMP, IL6, TGFβ); L1 dual-luciferase retrotransposition reporter.
- **Perturbations**: LLPS inhibitor 1,6-hexanediol (1.5%, 5 min); LINE-1 reverse-transcriptase inhibitor nevirapine (NVR, 300 μmol/L in vitro; 50 mg/kg/day in vivo); cGAS inhibitor RU.521 (10 μmol/L in vitro; 5 mg/kg in vivo); HMGN2 shRNA; anti–PD-1 antibody; Sting−/− mice.
- **In vivo**: KLN205OV-L1Md / OV-NC subcutaneous tumors in B6D2F1 mice; LLC in C57BL/6 (incl. Sting−/−); NVR, RU.521, combination, and cGAS inhibitor + anti–PD-1 regimens; flow cytometry of MDSC/Treg/exhausted-CD8 compartments; tissue cytokine profiling.

## Open Questions

- **Is HMGN2, a canonical nuclear chromatin protein, acting in the cytoplasm here, and how is it redistributed?** Its cytoplasmic role in scaffolding L1-ORF1p condensates is mechanistically novel and unresolved.
- **What distinguishes chronic noncanonical NF-κB (IL6) from acute IRF3/IFN-I output?** The paper invokes compartment-specific STING phosphorylation but does not directly dissect the switch in LUSC.
- **How specific is the IDR/C-CCD condensate trigger to pL1HS DNA:RNA hybrids vs other cytosolic nucleic acids,** and does ORF2p (reverse transcriptase) contribute to generating the hybrid trigger beyond NVR sensitivity?
- **Will LINE-1 + cGAS (or cGAS + anti–PD-1) combinations translate clinically,** given cGAS-STING's dual anti-/pro-tumor roles and the lack of a direct LLPS-targeting drug beyond 1,6-hexanediol?
