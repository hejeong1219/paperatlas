# ZCCHC3 is a stress granule zinc knuckle protein that strongly suppresses LINE-1 retrotransposition

_PLoS Genetics, 2023._

## Summary

LINE-1 (L1) elements are the only autonomously active human retrotransposons, and the cell deploys an arsenal of host restriction factors to keep them in check. This paper characterizes **ZCCHC3** (Zinc Finger CCHC-Type Containing 3), a poorly studied gag-like **zinc knuckle protein** previously implicated in the cGAS/STING and RIG-I/MDA5 innate antiviral response, and shows it is also a potent suppressor of human retrotransposition. Through co-immunoprecipitation, ZCCHC3 is shown to multimerize and to associate **RNA-dependently with the L1 ORF1p ribonucleoprotein (RNP)**, binding endogenous ORF1p and L1 RNA. Overexpression of tagged ZCCHC3 reduces cell-culture L1 retrotransposition to as little as 3% of vector control (and mouse IAP LTR retrotransposition to 13%), while shRNA knockdown raises L1 retrotransposition ~3.5-fold; the restriction is only partly dependent on the zinc knuckle domain and does not reduce L1 mRNA levels (i.e., not via RNA degradation). The central granule finding: ZCCHC3 is a **bona fide stress granule (SG) protein** — under sodium-arsenite oxidative stress it forms cytoplasmic granules that colocalize with canonical SG markers (eIF3η, TIA-1, G3BP2), strictly depends on G3BP1/2 for assembly (no granules in G3BP1/2 double-KO cells), and **strongly colocalizes with L1 ORF1p in those SGs**. The work further connects ZCCHC3 to the antiviral/anti-retroelement restriction factors **MOV10** and **ZAP/ZC3HAV1** (RNA-dependent and RNA-independent co-IPs respectively, plus colocalization with these and TRIM25 in SGs), and to the cytoplasmic **RNA exosome** (binding EXOSC5 and weakly DIS3L, cosedimentation in high-molecular-weight complexes that disperses on EXOSC3 knockdown). The picture is of ZCCHC3 acting within a stress-granule-resident antiviral/anti-retrotransposon RNP that may bridge ORF1p, ZAP/MOV10, and the cytoplasmic exosome.

## Key Points

- **ZCCHC3 multimerizes and associates with the L1 ORF1p RNP RNA-dependently.** FLAG- and V5-tagged ZCCHC3 co-IP each other (with a ~95 kD multimer band); ZCCHC3-FL co-IPs untagged ORF1p from a full-length L1 (pc-L1-RP), and tagged ORF1p co-IPs V5-TEV-ZCCHC3 — both interactions lost/diminished by RNase. Endogenous ZCCHC3 co-IPs endogenous ORF1p in an RNA-dependent manner.
- **The zinc knuckle domain contributes to, but is not strictly required for, ORF1p/L1-RNA binding.** Binding of ORF1p to the zinc-finger mutant ZCCHC3-FL-7AA was reduced 2-fold vs wild type, and associated L1 ORF1 RNA was reduced ~50%, indicating the zinc knuckle domain partly mediates the L1 RNP association.
- **ZCCHC3 overexpression strongly suppresses L1 retrotransposition.** In the 99-PUR-RPS-EGFP cell-culture assay, V5-TEV-ZCCHC3 and ZCCHC3-FL reduced L1 retrotransposition to **10% and 3%** of empty-vector control. Zinc-knuckle mutants 3AA/7AA only modestly restored it (to 11% and 20%), and an N-terminal deletion (Δ55–210) to 56% — so restriction is "only partly dependent upon its zinc knuckle domain."
- **ZCCHC3 also restricts an LTR retrotransposon.** Overexpression of ZCCHC3-FL in HeLa-JVM cells reduced mouse IAP LTR-element retrotransposition to **13%** of vector control.
- **Loss of ZCCHC3 (or ZAP) de-represses retrotransposition.** Two ZCCHC3 shRNAs increased L1 retrotransposition ~3.5-fold; CRISPR ZAP-KO 293T lines showed >2-fold increase, similar to ZCCHC3-depleted cells.
- **Restriction is not through degrading L1 RNA.** ZCCHC3-FL overexpression significantly reduced endogenous ORF1p to 66% of control (p<0.01), but neither overexpression nor knockdown of ZCCHC3 significantly changed endogenous L1 mRNA levels (three primer pairs across 5'UTR/ORF1/ORF2) — "ZCCHC3 protein may inhibit L1 RNPs by means other than RNA degradation."
- **ZCCHC3 is a bona fide stress granule protein.** Endogenous and tagged ZCCHC3 are diffusely cytoplasmic in unstressed cells, but sodium-arsenite oxidative stress drives them into large cytoplasmic granules colocalizing with canonical SG markers **eIF3η and TIA-1** (confirmed with two independent ZCCHC3 antibodies) and with **G3BP2**.
- **ZCCHC3 SG entry requires G3BP1/2.** In U2OS cells, SG formation rose from 1.7% (unstressed) to 90% (arsenite) for WT; but in G3BP1/2 double-KO (U2OS-DKO) cells only 1.6% formed ZCCHC3 granules even under stress, and endogenous ZCCHC3 likewise failed to granulate — "ZCCHC3 cytoplasmic granules are true SGs." The zinc-finger mutant 7AA reduced granule formation to 60% of WT.
- **ZCCHC3 colocalizes with L1 ORF1p in stress granules.** Endogenous ZCCHC3 and ORF1p "strongly colocalize in granules under oxidative stress" in 2102Ep cells; ZCCHC3 enters at least a subset of ORF1p cytoplasmic granules in multiple stressed lines.
- **ORF1p granules are not all stress granules.** Unlike ZCCHC3, overexpressed ORF1-EGFP-L1-RP formed granules in 40% of unstressed cells (75% stressed) and still formed granules in G3BP1/2 DKO cells where ZCCHC3 cannot — implying some ORF1p aggregates are not SGs (candidates discussed: P-bodies, "L1 bodies," exosome granules).
- **ZCCHC3 associates with MOV10 and ZAP, two known L1 restriction factors.** Tagged/endogenous ZCCHC3 co-IPs MOV10 (RNA-dependent) and ZAP (RNA-independent), and V5-TEV-ZCCHC3 colocalizes with endogenous MOV10 and ZAP in arsenite-induced granules; ZCCHC3 also colocalizes with TRIM25 in SGs.
- **ZAP stabilizes ZCCHC3.** In all five ZAP-KO 293T lines, endogenous ZCCHC3 protein was significantly reduced (~60%, p<0.01) vs non-KO controls, suggesting ZCCHC3 is stabilized when in complex with ZAP and that ZCCHC3/ZAP/TRIM25/MOV10 may form a common antiviral/anti-retrotransposon complex.
- **ZCCHC3 links to the cytoplasmic RNA exosome.** ZCCHC3-FL co-IPs exosome core subunit **EXOSC5** (RNase-resistant) and weakly the cytoplasmic catalytic subunit **DIS3L** (isoform 2), but not SKI complex subunits, EXOSC10, DIS3, EXOSC3/7/8; ZCCHC3 colocalizes with EXOSC5 in SGs. By sucrose velocity-gradient centrifugation ZCCHC3 cosediments in HMW fractions overlapping MOV10, ZAP, and DIS3, and EXOSC3 knockdown (shEXOSC3) shifts ZCCHC3 to LMW fractions — "indicating its dissociation from HMW complex, presumably the exosome." The authors hypothesize ZCCHC3 bridges cytoplasmic exosome components (via EXOSC5), perhaps with ZAP.

## Methods

- **Co-immunoprecipitation** in HEK 293T cells of FLAG/V5-TEV-tagged ZCCHC3 (and mutants 3AA, 7AA, Δ55–210, S363A) with L1 constructs (pc-L1-RP, pc-L1-1FH), ORF1p (α-ORF1-4H1, α-L1Hs-ORF1p), MOV10, ZAP (ZAP-L/PARP13.1), TRIM25, and exosome/SKI subunits; ±RNase A/DNase-free RNase to test RNA dependence; endogenous-protein IPs with α-ZCCHC3-CS antibody on Protein G Dynabeads.
- **RNA immunoprecipitation + RT-qPCR (RIP)** to quantify L1 (5'UTR-L1Hs, N51/ORF1, N22/ORF2), β-actin and GAPDH RNA bound by ZCCHC3-FL vs ZCCHC3-FL-7AA, MOV10, ZAP.
- **Cell-culture retrotransposition assays**: EGFP-based L1 assay (99-PUR-RPS-EGFP, flow cytometry) in 293T with ZCCHC3/ZCCHC7 overexpression and ZCCHC3 shRNA knockdown; mouse IAP neomycin-resistance assay (IAP-neoTNF) in HeLa-JVM; CRISPR ZAP-KO 293T clonal lines (crRNAs vs ZC3HAV1).
- **Cytotoxicity controls** (trypan blue exclusion; MultiTox-Fluor Multiplex) to rule out ZCCHC3-overexpression toxicity.
- **Immunofluorescence / confocal microscopy** (Nikon Eclipse Ti-A1) for SG markers eIF3η, TIA-1, G3BP2, plus ORF1p, MOV10, ZAP, TRIM25, EXOSC5/3, DIS3/DIS3L colocalization; SGs induced with 0.25 mM sodium arsenite (50 min); G3BP1/2 double-KO (U2OS-DKO) vs WT U2OS, 2102Ep, nTERA-2, 293T lines.
- **Western blotting** with ImageJ band quantification (endogenous ORF1p levels, ZCCHC3 levels in ZAP-KO lines).
- **Sucrose velocity-sedimentation centrifugation** (discontinuous 10–50% gradients) of 293T lysates ± ZCCHC3-FL ± shEXOSC3 to assess HMW-complex cosedimentation with exosome subunits (DIS3, EXOSC3, MTR4), MOV10, ZAP.
- **Mass spectrometry** (Johns Hopkins facility, Byonic v3.6.0) to map ZCCHC3 phosphosites (S66, S72, S363).

## Open Questions

- **How does ZCCHC3 mechanistically suppress L1 if not by degrading L1 RNA?** Restriction reduces ORF1p protein and retrotransposition without changing L1 mRNA levels; whether it blocks ORF1p RNP function, translation, or nuclear import is unresolved.
- **Is the zinc knuckle domain essential or only contributory?** This study finds restriction "only partly dependent" on the zinc knuckle (point mutations), whereas a concurrent paper (Zhang et al.) deleting 103 C-terminal residues found it essential — the discrepancy (point mutation vs truncation, structural integrity) is unsettled.
- **Does ZCCHC3 recruit the cytoplasmic exosome to L1/viral RNA?** By analogy to ZAP→EXOSC5, the authors hypothesize ZCCHC3 bridges cytoplasmic exosome components (EXOSC5/DIS3L) but did not directly test exosome-dependent L1 RNA turnover.
- **Do ZCCHC3, ZAP, TRIM25 and MOV10 act in a single physical complex?** All four (plus G3BP1) co-occur in the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid interactome and colocalize in SGs, but the stoichiometry and whether they form one functional anti-retroelement complex is proposed, not proven.
- **What is the identity of the non-SG ORF1p granules?** Some ORF1p aggregates form in G3BP1/2-DKO cells (where ZCCHC3 cannot) and persist through mitosis; whether these are P-bodies, "L1 bodies," or exosome granules remains open.
