# The structural basis of Edc3- and Scd6-mediated activation of the Dcp1:Dcp2 mRNA decapping complex

_The EMBO Journal, 2012._

DOI: [10.1038/emboj.2011.408](https://doi.org/10.1038/emboj.2011.408)

## Summary

The Dcp1:Dcp2 complex catalyses removal of the mRNA 5′ cap, the committed step that withdraws a transcript from translation, and its activity is modulated by activator proteins including Edc3 (enhancer of decapping 3). Using NMR spectroscopy and structure determination, Fromm et al solved the solution structure of the *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* Edc3 LSm (Like-Sm) domain bound to a short helical leucine-rich motif (HLM) derived from Dcp2 (HLM-1, residues 257–266). The HLM, disordered in isolation, folds into an amphipathic α-helix that engages the monomeric Edc3 LSm domain on a noncanonical surface, in a mode unprecedented for LSm–protein interactions. Guided by the structure, the authors identified additional HLMs in the intrinsically disordered C-terminal extension of Dcp2 (HLM-C1 to HLM-C5) that also bind Edc3. The LSm domain of the Edc3-related protein Scd6 (Sum2/Tral) competes with Edc3 for the very same HLMs, but with lower affinity. Both Edc3 and Scd6 stimulate Dcp1:Dcp2 decapping in vitro, an effect that requires HLM-1 and is proposed to arise from preventing the decapping complex from adopting its inactive closed conformation. The Edc3 dimer can bridge two decapping complexes, and the C-terminal HLMs of Dcp2 are required for localization of Dcp1:Dcp2 to P-bodies in vivo (though dispensable for P-body formation itself). In metazoans the HLM is found in Dcp1 (motif-1) rather than Dcp2, indicating the regulatory details changed during evolution while the core interaction is conserved.

## Key Points

- **Solution structure of the Edc3 LSm domain bound to an HLM.** The *S. pombe* Edc3 LSm domain folds into a canonical five-stranded β-sheet preceded by a short α-helical turn and is monomeric in solution (like the *D. melanogaster* and human orthologues). The bound Dcp2 segment (residues 257–266; identified by pull-downs as requiring residues 255–266) folds into an amphipathic α-helix only upon binding.
- **Noncanonical binding surface on a monomeric LSm domain.** The HLM contacts a surface formed by the Edc3 N-terminal α-helix (F6), β-strand 2 (F28), the β4–β5 loop (T49, I52), and β-strand 5 (D54, L55, I57). The four key Dcp2 helix residues are S257, L260, L261, L264 (motif SxxLLxxL), with the leucine methyls driving tight binding; the L260A/L264A double mutant abolishes the interaction.
- **A novel LSm interaction mode.** This mode differs from the two known LSm uses (β4–β5 ring contacts and RNA binding). It is enabled by two features: the monomeric nature of the domain frees β-strand 5 (normally used for inter-monomer contacts) to bind the N-terminal half of the HLM helix, and the unusually short N-terminal helix exposes a surface that contacts the C-terminal half of the HLM helix.
- **Multiple HLMs in the disordered Dcp2 C-terminal extension.** Beyond HLM-1, the predicted-disordered C-terminus (residues 553–741) contains at least five conserved motifs, HLM-C1 to HLM-C5. Pull-downs show HLM-C2 binds Edc3 as strongly as HLM-1 (HLM-C1 weaker); NMR additionally detects HLM-C5 binding. HLM-C3 and HLM-C4 do not bind, correlating with their lack of helical propensity.
- **Edc3 dimers can cluster decapping complexes.** Full-length Edc3 (which dimerizes via its YjeF-N domain) bridges two Dcp1:Dcp2 complexes simultaneously, shown by co-purification of His-tagged and untagged Dcp1; the isolated YjeF-N domain does not contact Dcp2. Together with the multiple HLMs, this implies a redundant, plastic network of Dcp2–Edc3 interactions.
- **Scd6 LSm domain competes with Edc3 for the same HLMs.** The Scd6 (Sum2) LSm domain binds the same motifs (HLM-1, C1, C2, C5) detected by Edc3, but more weakly (too weak to co-purify; seen only by NMR). NMR competition assays show stepwise addition of the tighter-binding Edc3 LSm domain releases HLM-1 from Scd6 — the interactions are mutually exclusive.
- **Both Edc3 and Scd6 stimulate decapping in vitro.** In single-turnover assays, the Edc3 LSm domain enhanced Dcp1:Dcp2 (residues 1–289) decapping; the Scd6 LSm domain also stimulated, though more weakly (reflecting lower affinity, used at 10-fold excess). Stimulation required HLM-1: it was lost when HLM-1 was deleted (Dcp2 1–243) or mutated (L260A/L264A).
- **Activation by destabilizing the inactive closed conformation.** Docking the Edc3:Dcp2 structure onto the closed (inactive) Dcp1:Dcp2 crystal form produces extensive steric clashes with the Dcp2 regulatory domain. Edc3/Scd6 binding to HLM-1 therefore requires the complex to open, shifting the structural equilibrium away from catalytically inactive states; the net activation is less than twofold.
- **C-terminal HLMs are required for P-body localization in vivo.** Fluorescence microscopy in *S. pombe* showed that deleting the C-terminal HLMs (HLM-C1–C5) abolished Dcp1–mCherry / Dcp2–GFP localization to cytoplasmic foci. A single HLM (HLM-1) is not sufficient for P-body targeting.
- **Decapping localization is dispensable for P-body formation.** Edc3–mCherry and Lsm7–mCherry still formed foci in the Dcp2 truncation strains, so Dcp1:Dcp2 is not required to build P-bodies. Removing all HLMs (including HLM-1) caused a slight temperature-dependent growth defect, consistent with loss of decapping regulation.
- **Evolutionary rewiring: HLM moves to Dcp1 in metazoans.** Yeast Dcp2 HLMs are absent in metazoan Dcp2. The conserved *D. melanogaster* Dcp1 "motif-1" (SIFNMLT) has helical propensity and binds the same Edc3 LSm surface by NMR, showing the basic interaction is conserved but relocated from Dcp2 to Dcp1.
- **Structural/biophysical toolkit.** Methods included NMR backbone/sidechain assignment and NOESY-based solution structure determination (XPLOR-NIH/TALOS), NMR titration and competition experiments, His6 and MBP pull-downs / co-purifications, in vitro single-turnover decapping assays with TLC readout of m7GDP, and fluorescence microscopy of fission yeast strains.

## Methods

- **NMR spectroscopy and structure calculation.** Spectra recorded at 30 °C on Bruker AVIII-600/800. Backbone/sidechain assignments via HNCA, HNCACB, CC(CO)NH-TOCSY, HNCO, HN(CA)CO, CCH-TOCSY. Distance restraints from six NOESY spectra (80-ms mixing); dihedral restraints from HNHB and TALOS; structures computed in XPLOR-NIH 2.9.4. Chemical shifts deposited in BMRB (18041, 18042); coordinates in PDB (Edc3 LSm domain: **4A53**; Edc3 LSm domain:Dcp2 complex: **4A54**).
- **Protein production.** Edc3, Scd6 (Sum2), Dcp1, Dcp2 cloned from *S. pombe* genomic DNA/cDNA into modified pET vectors; expressed in *E. coli* BL21(DE3) Codon Plus RIL (15N/13C labelling in M9). Purified by Ni-NTA, TEV cleavage, and size-exclusion chromatography.
- **Interaction assays.** His6-tagged and MBP-tagged pull-downs / co-purifications to map Dcp2 regions and HLMs; NMR titrations to detect chemical-shift perturbations; NMR competition assays between Edc3 and Scd6 LSm domains.
- **Decapping assays.** Single-turnover conditions with 0.05 µM Dcp1:Dcp2 ± 10-fold molar excess Edc3 or Scd6 LSm domain; 32P-cap-labelled RNA substrate; reaction at 30 °C for 5 min, stopped with EDTA; released m7GDP quantified by TLC (activity relative to unstimulated complex; n=3).
- **In vivo localization.** *S. pombe* strains expressing dcp2+–GFP or truncated dcp2 (Δ553–741, Δ290–741, Δ244–741) from the endogenous locus, combined with dcp1+–mCherry, edc3+–mCherry, or lsm7+–mCherry, made by PCR-based gene targeting and imaged by fluorescence microscopy.

## Open Questions

- Do decapping complexes assembled with different HLM/activator occupancy (variable Edc3/Scd6 content) differ in substrate specificity, as the authors speculate?
- Does the Edc3-mediated clustering of decapping complexes via multiple HLMs contribute directly to P-body assembly, and to what extent quantitatively?
- Which individual mRNA degradation rates in vivo are controlled by HLM-1-dependent modulation of decapping activity, and how does this map onto the temperature-dependent growth defect of the all-HLM-deletion strain?
- Are additional HLM-bearing partners (e.g., Rps28B, with an HLM-like EDDILVLM motif) recruited to the decapping machinery via the second LSm domain of dimeric Edc3?
- In metazoans, does Edc3/Scd6 LSm binding to Dcp1 motif-1 enhance catalytic activity by inducing Dcp2 conformational changes analogous to the yeast mechanism? (No Dcp1:Ge-1:Dcp2 structure is yet available.)
- How does the Scd6 dual function — translational repression plus newly demonstrated decapping stimulation — integrate at the level of specific transcripts, given the crosstalk also seen for Pat1 and Dhh1?
