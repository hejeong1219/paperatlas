# Candidate mechanisms of acquired resistance to first-line osimertinib in EGFR-mutated advanced non-small cell lung cancer

This pre-specified exploratory FLAURA analysis shows that first-line osimertinib resistance in EGFR-mutant advanced NSCLC is only partly explained by recurrent plasma ctDNA alterations: MET amplification and EGFR C797S are visible, but 65% of osimertinib-arm patients in the acquired-resistance subset had no detectable candidate genomic mechanism.

## Key Points

- FLAURA randomized 556 previously untreated EGFR-mutant locally advanced/metastatic NSCLC patients to first-line osimertinib 80 mg daily or comparator EGFR-TKI gefitinib/erlotinib.
- The overall FLAURA outcomes cited in this paper favored osimertinib: PFS 18.9 versus 10.2 months and OS 38.6 versus 31.8 months.
- The acquired-resistance plasma analysis required paired baseline and progression/treatment-discontinuation plasma plus detectable baseline plasma EGFR mutation, yielding 109 osimertinib-arm and 145 comparator-arm patients.
- In the osimertinib arm, 38/109 patients (35%) had a detectable acquired resistance mechanism and 71/109 (65%) had no detectable candidate mechanism in plasma.
- No acquired EGFR T790M was detected after first-line osimertinib.
- The most common osimertinib-arm acquired events were MET amplification (17/109, 16%) and EGFR mutations (11/109, 10%), including EGFR C797S in 7/109 (6%).
- Comparator EGFR-TKI resistance was dominated by EGFR T790M: 64/145 (44%), with MET amplification in 9/145 (6%) and CDK6 amplification in 6/145 (4%).
- Baseline tissue genomics nominated a negative-prognostic set: TP53 missense plus alterations in RBM10, HER2, MET, SMARCA4, or RICTOR were enriched in suboptimal response tumors.

## Study Design

- Parent trial: FLAURA, randomized double-blind phase III study, NCT02296125.
- Disease: previously untreated EGFR-mutated locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with sensitizing Exon 19 deletion or L858R.
- Treatment arms: osimertinib 80 mg once daily (n = 279) versus comparator EGFR-TKI gefitinib 250 mg once daily or erlotinib 150 mg once daily (n = 277).
- Plasma design: serial plasma at baseline, early on-treatment timepoints, every 6 weeks, progression, and/or treatment discontinuation. This analysis compared paired baseline and progression/discontinuation plasma up to March 2019.
- Plasma assay: Guardant360 74-gene panel or GuardantOMNI 500-gene panel; reported genes were restricted to those represented across both panels.
- Tissue design: baseline screening tumor tissue analyzed by FoundationOne CDx when sufficient tissue and consent were available.
- Suboptimal response definition: best overall response of stable/progressive disease with PFS <6 months, or failure to clear plasma ctDNA by ddPCR at 6 weeks.

## Quantitative Findings

### Plasma ctDNA resistance subset

- Paired plasma was available for 137/279 (49%) patients in the osimertinib arm and 179/277 (65%) in the comparator arm.
- Detectable baseline plasma EGFR mutation was present in 109/137 (80%) osimertinib-arm paired samples and 145/179 (81%) comparator-arm paired samples.
- The acquired-resistance analysis subset therefore covered 254 total patients with paired plasma and detectable baseline plasma EGFR mutation.

### Osimertinib acquired resistance

- Candidate mechanism detected: 38/109 (35%).
- No detectable candidate mechanism: 71/109 (65%).
- MET amplification: 17/109 (16%).
- EGFR C797S: 7/109 (6%); one case had co-occurring C797S and C797N.
- Other EGFR alterations included L718Q, L718V, G796S, and S768I.
- MAPK/PI3K pathway alterations included BRAF V600E, KRAS A146T/G12C/G12D, and PIK3CA E453K/E545K/H1047R.
- Cell-cycle amplifications included CCND1, CCND2, CCND3, CCNE1, CDK4, and CDK6.
- Single acquired mechanisms were seen in 23/109 (21%); multiple mechanisms were seen in 15/109 (14%), or 39% of patients with any detected mechanism.
- There was no clear global relationship between mechanism type and duration of osimertinib treatment, but MET amplification appeared in 11 of the 15 shortest osimertinib treatment durations.

### Comparator EGFR-TKI acquired resistance

- Candidate mechanism detected: 71/145 (49%).
- No detectable candidate mechanism: 74/145 (51%).
- EGFR T790M: 64/145 (44%).
- MET amplification: 9/145 (6%).
- CDK6 amplification: 6/145 (4%).
- Single acquired mechanisms: 57/145 (39%), mostly EGFR T790M.
- Multiple mechanisms: 14/145 (10%), or 20% of patients with any detected acquired mechanism.

### Baseline tissue genomics and suboptimal response

- Baseline tissue NGS had sufficient tissue/consent in 147 patients; 104 had pass/qualified results (46 osimertinib, 58 comparator).
- Frequent baseline alterations included TP53 (62%), EGFR amplification (20%), RB1 (12%), RBM10 (5%), HER2 (3%), MET (3%), SMARCA4 (3%), and RICTOR (3%).
- TP53 alterations split into missense (28/64, 44%) and loss-of-function classes (36/64, 56%).
- The negative-prognostic set TP53 missense, RBM10, HER2, MET, SMARCA4, or RICTOR appeared in 20/23 (87%) suboptimal response tumors versus 18/81 (22%) non-suboptimal tumors; Fisher exact test P < 0.001.
- EGFR amplification and any TP53 mutation status, alone or with RB1, were not significantly enriched in suboptimal response tumors.

## Relevance to Drug-Response POC

- This paper is the cleanest warning label for an SNV/ctDNA-only drug-response model: even in a genotype-defined, targeted-therapy trial, most first-line osimertinib resistance in the plasma subset had no detected recurrent genomic mechanism.
- For a user dataset with somatic SNV plus global proteome/phosphoproteome, the useful framing is: first quantify what the SNV block explains, then ask whether protein abundance and kinase/phosphosite activity fill the unexplained resistance fraction.
- For targeted therapy, this supports a model ladder of `driver alteration / ctDNA mechanism -> target protein abundance -> target or bypass phospho activity -> non-genetic state`, rather than treating mutation status as a sufficient resistance explanation.
- Baseline TP53 missense, RBM10, HER2, MET, SMARCA4, and RICTOR also suggest a compact SNV/pathway feature set for the first POC, but the paper's central lesson is that many resistant cases will remain unassigned without non-genomic layers.

## Limitations and Caveats

- The acquired-resistance analysis used plasma ctDNA because progression tissue was not available; histologic transformation, protein expression changes, and other non-genetic resistance states were not evaluated.
- Plasma ctDNA can underestimate amplification events, so MET amplification frequency may be higher in tissue than the reported 16%.
- Patients without detectable baseline plasma EGFR mutation were excluded from the resistance analysis subset.
- The analyses were exploratory/descriptive and based on post-baseline subsets; they should not be treated as a complete resistance taxonomy.
- This is not a proteome/phosphoproteome paper. It should guide POC framing and SNV-block caveats, not be counted as a core phosphoproteomic response training template.
