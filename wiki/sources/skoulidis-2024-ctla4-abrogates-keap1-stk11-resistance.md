# CTLA4 blockade abrogates KEAP1/STK11-related resistance to PD-(L)1 inhibitors

This study revisits one of the hardest checkpoint-resistant lung cancer genotypes and argues that `CTLA-4` co-blockade can partially bypass the `STK11` or `KEAP1` resistance program that limits PD-(L)1 efficacy.

## Key Points

- Skoulidis et al. focus on lung cancers with `KEAP1` and `STK11` alterations, genomic contexts repeatedly associated with poor outcomes on PD-(L)1 monotherapy.
- The central claim is not that the genotype ceases to matter, but that adding CTLA-4 blockade can alter the immune bottleneck enough to recover clinically meaningful activity in this resistant molecular subgroup.
- This is important because it turns a negative biomarker into a treatment-selection problem: the lesion may predict monotherapy failure without predicting universal immunotherapy futility.
- In the wiki's broader logic, this paper serves as a rescue-study counterpart to the 2018 STK11/LKB1 resistance paper, showing how mechanism-informed combinations may be more useful than abandoning immunotherapy outright.
- The work also supports a larger theme running through this corpus: resistance biomarkers are most valuable when they point toward a reversible or bypassable axis.

## Study Context (as described in the PDF text)

- Clinical context emphasized: advanced non-squamous NSCLC treated with platinum chemotherapy + PD-(L)1 inhibitor regimens, and the added value of dual immune checkpoint blockade (`PD-(L)1` + `CTLA-4`).
- Trial anchor named in the text: randomized phase III `POSEIDON` (durvalumab ± tremelimumab added to chemotherapy), used to test whether dual ICB adds benefit in `STK11` and/or `KEAP1` altered tumors.
- Retrospective cohort described: a multi-centre cohort of 871 molecularly annotated advanced nsNSCLC treated with standard chemo-immunotherapy including pembrolizumab (`PCP`; `n = 439` mentioned for the PCP subset).

## Key Results (numbers visible in extracted PDF text)

- In the retrospective `PCP` cohort figures, `KEAP1` mutant tumors show substantially worse outcomes than `KEAP1` wild-type tumors:
  - Median PFS: 2.7 months (KEAP1 mutant; n=42) vs 5.7 months (KEAP1 WT; n=102).
  - Median OS: 7.6 months (KEAP1 mutant; n=42) vs 16.6 months (KEAP1 WT; n=103).
  - Multivariable hazard ratios in the figure text: PFS HR 2.07 (95% CI 1.35–3.17) and OS HR 2.24 (95% CI 1.42–3.54), log-rank P < 0.0001.
- The abstract framing states that in `POSEIDON`, patients with `STK11` and/or `KEAP1` mutations derived clinical benefit from dual ICB (durvalumab + tremelimumab) added to chemotherapy, but not from durvalumab alone added to chemotherapy.

## Mechanistic Claim (immune bottleneck shift)

- The abstract text links `KEAP1`/`STK11` alterations to an adverse microenvironment characterized by suppressive myeloid predominance and CD8 depletion, with relative sparing of CD4 effector subsets.
- Dual ICB is described as engaging CD4 effector cells and reprogramming the myeloid compartment toward iNOS-expressing tumoricidal phenotypes, contributing (with CD4 and CD8 T cells) to antitumor efficacy.
