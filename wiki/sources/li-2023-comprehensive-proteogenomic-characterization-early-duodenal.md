# Comprehensive proteogenomic characterization of early duodenal cancer reveals the carcinogenesis tracks of different subtypes.

_Nature communications, 2023._ PMID: [36991000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36991000/).

DOI: [10.1038/s41467-023-37221-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37221-5)

## Summary

The subtypes of duodenal cancer (DC) are complicated and the carcinogenesis process is not well characterized. We present comprehensive characterization of 438 samples from 156 DC patients, covering 2 major and 5 rare subtypes. Proteogenomics reveals LYN amplification at the chromosome 8q gain functioned in the transmit from intraepithelial neoplasia phase to infiltration tumor phase via MAPK signaling, and illustrates the DST mutation improves mTOR signaling in the duodenal adenocarcinoma stage. Proteome-based analysis elucidates stage-specific molecular characterizations and carcinogenesis tracks, and defines the cancer-driving waves of the adenocarcinoma and Brunner's gland subtypes. The drug-targetable alanyl-tRNA synthetase (AARS1) in the high tumor mutation burden/immune infiltration is significantly enhanced in DC progression, and catalyzes the lysine-alanylation of poly-ADP-ribose polymerases (PARP1), which decreases the apoptosis of cancer cells, eventually promoting cell proliferation and tumorigenesis. We assess the proteogenomic landscape of early DC, and provide insights into the molecular features corresponding therapeutic targets.

## Multi-Omics Identification Extraction

- Cohort/scope: 438 samples from 156 early duodenal cancer patients; proteomics across all 438 samples and phosphoproteomics on 111 samples from 49 patients.
- Proteome: 14,426 gene products / protein groups identified in 438 samples using match-between-runs; 11,904 remained after stricter peptide/keratin filtering; 7,400 were used for PCA/analysis after stage-coverage filtering.
- Phosphoproteome: 38,696 phosphosites corresponding to 9,716 phosphoproteins identified; 12,851 phosphosites identified in more than 20% of samples in every stage were used for cohort analysis.
- Acetylome: no separate acetylome dataset; acetylation appears as protein N-terminal variable modification during database searching, not as an acetyl-proteome layer. The biology section focuses on lysine alanylation of PARP1 rather than acetylome profiling.
- MS method: label-free proteomics with iBAQ/FOT quantification; Fe-NTA phosphopeptide enrichment for phosphoproteomics; Firmiana/Mascot search against NCBI RefSeq.
- Instrument/platform: EASY-nLC 1200 coupled to Q Exactive HF-X Hybrid Quadrupole-Orbitrap for proteome/phosphoproteome LC-MS/MS; separate MALDI-TOF/TOF SCIEX-5800 was used for in vitro alanylation/de-alanylation assays.
- Extraction evidence: local PDF Results and Methods report 14,426 protein groups, 38,696 phosphosites / 9,716 phosphoproteins, Fe-NTA enrichment, Firmiana/Mascot, iBAQ/FOT, EASY-nLC 1200, and Q Exactive HF-X.
- Interpretation note: use 14,426 and 38,696 for identification-scale plots; show 7,400 and 12,851 as filtered downstream-analysis counts when caveats are displayed.

## Key Points

- The study maps early duodenal cancer progression across major and rare subtypes using proteogenomics.
- LYN amplification/MAPK signaling and DST mutation/mTOR signaling are prominent proteogenomic findings.
- AARS1-related lysine alanylation of PARP1 is a separate PTM mechanism, but the paper does not provide a global acetylome dataset.
