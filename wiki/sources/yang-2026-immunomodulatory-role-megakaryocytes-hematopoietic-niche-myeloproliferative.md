# Immunomodulatory role of megakaryocytes in the hematopoietic niche of myeloproliferative neoplasms

_Haematologica, 2026._

## Summary

Using an aging murine model of myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) with megakaryocyte (MK)-restricted JAK2V617F expression (Pf4-cre+FF1+), this study positions diseased MK as active immune regulators of the bone marrow niche rather than passive platelet precursors. Compared to wild-type MK, aged JAK2V617F mutant MK exhibit enhanced exogenous-antigen uptake/processing and MHC class I presentation, secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines (PF4, TGFβ, IL-1β), and drive T-cell dysregulation (expanded but hypofunctional CD8+ T cells, reduced Treg) in the marrow. Single-cell RNA-seq shows that aging amplifies a JAK2V617F MK–driven inflammatory/immune-activation program (interferon-γ, interferon-α, TNFα/NF-κB), and in chimeric mice with co-existing wild-type and mutant cells the heightened MK immune signature tracks with mutant-cell burden and MPN phenotype (and is not explained by the JAK2V617F mutation alone — Mouse C vs Mouse B). Mechanistically, the authors implicate the LINE-1 (long interspersed element-1) retrotransposon as an upstream innate-immune trigger: aged mutant MK markedly upregulate LINE-1 transcripts (5′UTR, ORF1, ORF2, 3′UTR) and the cytosolic DNA sensors cGAS and STING, the LINE-1 RNA increase is reduced by the reverse-transcriptase inhibitor lamivudine, and LINE-1-encoded ORF1p protein is detected by IHC in marrow MK of 12/13 human MPN patients but none of 5 orthopedic controls. The work frames LINE-1 activation as a candidate driver of chronic marrow inflammation and a targetable mechanism in clonal hematopoiesis and MPN progression. There is no P-body / mRNA-decapping content in this paper; its relevance to the p-body corpus is via the shared immune-ISG and LINE-1/retrotransposon-sensing axes (cGAS–STING, ORF1p), not via cytoplasmic mRNA granules.

## Key Points

- **MK-restricted JAK2V617F model.** Pf4-cre+FF1+ mice (Cre-inducible human JAK2V617F driven off the human JAK2 promoter, restricted to the MK lineage) develop an essential-thrombocythemia-like phenotype (modest neutrophilia, sustained thrombocytosis over 2 years). RT-PCR confirmed the human JAK2 transgene is expressed in MK but **not** in long-term HSC, short-term HSC, MPP2, MPP3, T cells, B cells, macrophages, or myeloid cells.
- **Aged mutant MK remodel the marrow immune landscape.** Aged (2-year-old) Pf4+FF1+ mice show a marked increase in marrow CD3+CD8+ T cells with a reduction in Treg cells — changes not seen in young mice. Despite their numeric increase, CD8+ T cells were functionally impaired (reduced intracellular IFNγ).
- **scRNA-seq: aging amplifies a mutant-MK immune program.** 47,730 cells (4 mice), 29 clusters. GSEA of MK showed interferon-γ response, interferon-α response, and TNFα signaling via NF-κB significantly upregulated in aged Pf4+FF1+ MK but **not** in young mice. No distinct "immune MK" cluster (Ccl3/Ccl4/Cd53/Lsp1/Cxcr4) was resolved, attributed to use of unfractionated marrow.
- **Enhanced antigen uptake and MHC I presentation.** Aged mutant marrow MK showed increased DQ-Ovalbumin uptake/proteolytic processing and Ova peptide presentation on MHC I vs wild-type MK. Validated in human iPSC-derived MK: JAK2V617F-mutant (PVB1.4) vs wild-type (BC1) MK, with increased Ova uptake and MHC I presentation in the mutant.
- **Antigen-specific CD8+ T-cell engagement.** Ova257-264 (MHC I-restricted)–loaded marrow MK enhanced OT-I CD8+ T-cell proliferation and transiently increased IFNγ (peaking day 2) and IFNγ ELISpot; Ova323-339 (MHC II)–loaded MK had no effect on OT-II CD4+ T cells. No WT-vs-mutant difference was seen with the potent Ova257-264 peptide (the peptide may mask functional differences).
- **Pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion.** ELISA of marrow plasma showed significantly increased PF4, TGFβ, and IL-1β in aged Pf4+FF1+ mice vs controls, with no difference in young mice. MK are the primary marrow source of PF4; PF4 is linked to immune-cell recruitment, monocyte differentiation, and CD8+ T-cell inhibition, so PF4 upregulation may contribute to MK-driven immune dysfunction.
- **MK immune signature tracks with mutant-cell burden, not mutation alone.** In Tie2-cre+FF1+ chimeric mice (wild-type CD45.1 + mutant CD45.2 marrow mixed 100:0 / 90:10 / 10:90), a "dose-dependent" MPN phenotype emerged. scRNA-seq (15,403 cells, 3 mice) showed proliferation/inflammation/immune-activation gene sets enriched in MK from the high-burden MPN mouse (Mouse C) vs both a low-burden non-MPN mouse (Mouse A) and a comparable-burden non-MPN mouse (Mouse B) — i.e. the immunomodulatory phenotype is **not solely attributable to the JAK2V617F mutation**.
- **Elevated LINE-1 transcription in aged mutant MK.** RT-qPCR with primers targeting active LINE-1 families showed significant increases in 5′UTR, ORF1, ORF2, and 3′UTR transcripts in mutant MK from aged (but not young) mice. The increase was MK-specific — not seen in CD3+ T, B220+ B, CD11b+ myeloid, or Lin-cKit+Sca1+ HSPC populations.
- **Lamivudine suppresses LINE-1 RNA.** Treating aged marrow MK with lamivudine (25 µM, 24 h), a reverse-transcriptase inhibitor, significantly reduced LINE-1 (ORF1/ORF2) transcripts in mutant MK, implicating retrotransposition activity in the elevated LINE-1 RNA (authors note this is likely an indirect RT-inhibition effect).
- **cGAS/STING upregulated in aged mutant MK.** The cytosolic innate-immune DNA sensors cGAS and STING (which induce interferon and interferon-regulated genes) are upregulated in aged Pf4+FF1+ mutant MK, linking LINE-1 activation to interferon/innate-immune signaling in the niche.
- **LINE-1 ORF1p protein in human MPN MK.** IHC (CD41 + ORF1p, validated on adult testis) detected ORF1p in marrow MK of **12 of 13** MPN patients (avg age 71) but in **0 of 5** orthopedic-surgery controls. Within patients, ORF1p-positive and -negative MK coexisted (40–100% MK positive in affected patients; ~70 MK evaluated/sample).
- **ORF1p spreads beyond MK in advanced disease.** Diffuse ORF1p in non-MK marrow cells appeared in 2 patients with disease progression — one transitioning PV→MDS (#SL025) and one with accelerated-phase post-PV myelofibrosis (#SL046) — suggesting LINE-1 activation extends beyond MK during evolution and may contribute to neoplastic clonal expansion.
- **Model / rationale for LINE-1 in MK.** LINE-1 is the only protein-coding transposon still active in humans; its retrotransposition peaks during DNA replication / cell cycling. Because MK undergo endomitosis (multiple rounds of DNA synthesis without division), the authors argue MK are particularly vulnerable to LINE-1 reactivation with aging.

## Methods

- **Mouse models:** JAK2V617F Flip-Flop (FF1) mice (Cre-inducible human JAK2V617F, human JAK2 promoter) crossed to Pf4-Cre (MK-restricted) → Pf4+FF1+; and Tie2-cre+FF1+ (JAK2V617F in all hematopoietic cells) used to build chimeras. No randomization/blinding.
- **Chimeric MPN model:** lethally irradiated CD45.1 wild-type recipients transplanted with mixed wild-type (CD45.1) + JAK2V617F mutant (CD45.2 Tie2-cre+FF1+) marrow at 100:0 / 90:10 / 10:90 ratios; blood counts, LSK/HSC enumeration at 16 weeks.
- **Single-cell RNA-seq:** unfractionated marrow from young (4-mo) and aged (1-yr) Pf4+FF1+ and control mice (Fig. 2) and from 3 chimeric mice (Fig. 4); UMAP clustering, manual cell-identity assignment (MK markers Pf4, Gp9, Mpl, vWF, Itga2b; HSPC markers CD34, Flt3, CD27), GSEA of hallmark pathways.
- **Immune-function assays:** flow cytometry of marrow T-cell subsets and intracellular IFNγ; DQ-Ovalbumin and Ova-AF647 antigen uptake/processing; anti–MHC-I-Ova presentation; OT-I/OT-II co-culture (CFSE proliferation, IFNγ); IFNγ ELISpot; targeted cytokine arrays; marrow-plasma ELISA (PF4, TGFβ, IL-1β).
- **Human iPSC MK:** JAK2V617F-homozygous (PVB1.4, MPN-patient) and wild-type (BC1, healthy) iPSC lines → spin-EB differentiation to CD34+CD45+ progenitors → CD41+CD42+ mature MK; genotypes confirmed by nested allele-specific PCR.
- **LINE-1 assays:** RT-qPCR with primers to 5′UTR/ORF1/ORF2/3′UTR of active LINE-1 families across flow-sorted marrow populations; lamivudine (25 µM, 24 h) RT-inhibition; cGAS/STING RT-qPCR.
- **Human pathology:** IHC for CD41 and ORF1p on archived marrow biopsies from 13 MPN patients and 5 age-matched orthopedic-surgery controls (ORF1p validated on adult testis); % ORF1p+ MK quantified (~70 MK/sample).

## Open Questions

- **How does JAK2V617F promote LINE-1 activation in MK?** The causal link from JAK/STAT signaling to retrotransposon derepression is unresolved (authors pose this explicitly).
- **Cause or consequence?** Is MK-driven inflammation (and LINE-1 activation) a driver/accelerator of MPN progression, or a downstream consequence of clonal expansion?
- **Functional contribution of cGAS–STING.** cGAS/STING upregulation is correlative here (RT-qPCR); whether the LINE-1 → cGAS–STING → interferon axis is *required* for the inflammatory niche phenotype was not tested by perturbation.
- **Does ORF1p spread reflect retrotransposition or just expression?** ORF1p protein in non-MK cells in advanced disease raises whether bona fide retrotransposition (not just LINE-1 RNA/protein) propagates during MPN evolution.
- **Therapeutic tractability.** Whether RT inhibition (lamivudine-class) or cGAS/STING modulation can dampen the inflammatory niche and slow clonal expansion in MPN is untested in vivo here.
