# Overcoming T cell tolerance to tumor self-antigens through catch-bond engineering

## Summary

본 연구는 central tolerance 때문에 약하게 반응하는 tumor self-antigen 특이적 T cell의 한계를 mechanical force 기반 catch-bond engineering으로 극복했습니다. nonmutated tumor-associated antigen인 prostatic acid phosphatase(PAP)에 특이적인 weakly reactive TCR을 시작점으로, catch-bonding "hotspot" 단일 잔기 mutation을 통해 TCR-pMHC bond lifetime을 증가시키면서 affinity와 fine specificity를 보존했습니다. 이 engineered TCR을 발현한 T cell은 종양 내 expansion, effector phenotype, tumor elimination이 현저히 향상되었고, crystal structure 및 molecular dynamics simulation은 catch-bond hotspot의 단일 mutation이 TCR-pMHC interface의 water 재배치로 peptide 결합을 prime한다는 메커니즘을 보였습니다.

## Key Points

- PAP-specific weakly reactive TCR을 시작 template으로 catch-bond engineering을 적용하여 TCR-pMHC bond lifetime을 늘리는 hotspot 단일 mutation을 식별했다.
- engineered TCR은 affinity 자체는 physiological 수준으로 유지하면서 antigen fine specificity도 보존하여 off-target reactivity 위험을 낮추었다.
- engineered TCR-T cell은 wild-type 대비 in vivo tumor 내 expansion, effector phenotype, tumor elimination이 vastly superior했다.
- crystal structure + molecular dynamics simulation으로 hotspot 단일 잔기 mutation이 TCR-pMHC interface의 water 재배치를 통해 peptide 결합을 prime하는 분자 메커니즘을 보였다.
- 시사점: central tolerance로 약하게 반응하던 self/shared-antigen 특이 TCR을 biophysical engineering으로 functional하게 강화할 수 있는 새로운 TCR-T therapy 전략을 제시한다.

## Abstract (PubMed)

T cells are often weakly responsive to tumor self-antigens because of central tolerance, constraining their ability to eliminate tumors. We exploited mechanical force to engineer a weakly reactive T cell receptor (TCR) specific for a nonmutated tumor-associated antigen (TAA), prostatic acid phosphatase (PAP). We identified a catch-bonding "hotspot" whose mutation enhanced T cell activity by increasing TCR-pMHC (peptide-major histocompatibility complex) bond lifetime while preserving physiological affinities and antigen fine specificities. T cells expressing these engineered TCRs showed vastly superior expansion in the tumor, effector phenotypes, and tumor elimination. Crystal structures and molecular dynamics simulations revealed a single amino acid mutation at the catch-bond hotspot primes the TCR for peptide interaction through water reorganization at the TCR-pMHC interface. Catch-bond engineering is a viable biophysically based strategy for transforming tolerized antitumor T cells into potent TCR-T cell therapy killers.
