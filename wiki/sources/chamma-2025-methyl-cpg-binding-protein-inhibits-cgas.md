# The methyl-CpG-binding protein 2 inhibits cGAS-associated signaling

_Nature Communications, 2025._

## Summary

The DNA sensor cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) detects cytosolic double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) and triggers type I interferon (IFN) and inflammatory responses, but the dsDNA-cGAS interaction step is rate-limiting and few of its regulators are known. This paper identifies the methyl-CpG-binding protein 2 (MeCP2) — a broadly expressed nuclear transcriptional regulator whose loss-of-function causes Rett syndrome (RTT) — as a negative regulator of dsDNA-associated inflammation. The authors show that cytosolic dsDNA triggers active, CRM1-dependent export of MeCP2 from the nucleus to the cytosol, where MeCP2 binds the same dsDNA molecules as cGAS and competes with it: in MeCP2-deficient cells cGAS recruitment to dsDNA, 2'3'-cGAMP production, STING/TBK1/IRF3 activation, and downstream IFN/ISG induction are all amplified. MeCP2 loss therefore enforces a chronic low-grade type I IFN antiviral state that restricts the dsDNA virus HSV-1 (but not the ssRNA virus VSV), a signature reproduced in MeCP2-deficient mouse liver/hippocampus and in RTT patient samples. A second consequence of MeCP2 nuclear export is the disruption of its canonical gene-silencing function: dsDNA stimulation de-represses endogenous retroelements, with a marked upregulation of LINE-1, increased ORF1p protein, and a ~10-fold accumulation of cytosolic LINE-1-derived DNA that feeds back to sustain cGAS-dependent signaling. Reverse-transcriptase inhibition (Tenofovir) blunts the persistence of the IFN response, nominating LINE-1/retroelement suppression and dsDNA-sensing pathways as therapeutic options in MeCP2 deficiency. The work links a chromatin regulator to innate dsDNA sensing through two arms: direct cytosolic dsDNA sequestration away from cGAS, and nuclear de-repression of immunogenic LINE-1.

## Key Points

- **MeCP2 binds immune-stimulatory cytosolic dsDNA but not dsRNA.** In vitro DNA pulldowns from WT-MEF and WT-RAW264.7 whole-cell extracts recovered both MeCP2 and cGas on 80-nt biotinylated dsDNA, with weaker binding to ssDNA and no binding to dsRNA; recruitment was independent of dsDNA sequence (two distinct 80-nt probes gave similar binding) (Fig. 1b-e).
- **dsDNA triggers MeCP2 relocalization to the cytosol.** dsDNA (but not ssDNA or dsRNA) transfection increased cytosolic MeCP2 and decreased nuclear MeCP2 by immunofluorescence, with significant cytosolic overlap (Pearson's) between MeCP2 and the dsDNA signal in WT-MEF (Fig. 1i-m); in-cell pulldowns confirmed MeCP2-dsDNA interaction in MEF, RAW264.7 and human THP-1 cells.
- **Cytosolic MeCP2 accumulation is driven by active nuclear export.** Time-course (3, 6, 16 h) showed progressive cytosolic accumulation; the CRM1 inhibitor Leptomycin B (20 nM) blocked dsDNA-induced MeCP2 export and retained MeCP2 in the nucleus (Fig. 2a-d). Export still occurred in cGas-knockout MEF, showing it is triggered by dsDNA itself, not by cGas-Sting signaling (Fig. 2e).
- **Endogenous cytosolic DNA is sufficient to drive MeCP2 export.** Trex1-deficient MEF (chronic cytosolic DNA from aberrant LINE-1 activity) showed cytosolic MeCP2 at steady state; the reverse-transcriptase inhibitor Tenofovir reduced cytosolic dsDNA, cGas and MeCP2 staining (Fig. 2f,g; Supplementary Fig. 2h-j).
- **MeCP2 and cGas bind the same dsDNA moieties and compete.** dsDNA increased cGas-MeCP2 colocalization (MEF^EGFP-cGAS), and the two co-immunoprecipitated upon dsDNA stimulation with similar binding kinetics (detectable 30 min post-transfection); FLAG-MeCP2/cGas interaction increased over time (Fig. 3a-e). Loss of cGas enhanced MeCP2 recruitment to dsDNA and reduced MeCP2 increased cGas recruitment to dsDNA — reciprocal competition (Fig. 3f,g); Sting was dispensable for either's recruitment.
- **MeCP2 inhibits cGas-dependent type I IFN responses.** In MEF^gMecp2 vs MEF^gCTRL, dsDNA stimulation produced higher cytosolic cGas, elevated intracellular 2'3'-cGAMP, and increased Ifnβ, Il6, Cxcl10, Ccl5 and Isg15; RAW264.7^gMecp2 showed heightened pTbk1, pSting and pStat1 (Fig. 4a-e). The H-151 Sting inhibitor decreased the inflammatory signature, confirming Sting-dependence (Fig. 4f).
- **MeCP2 is a specific, dose-dependent brake on dsDNA-cGAS signaling.** MeCP2 overexpression (eMeCP2) before dsDNA reduced Ifnβ, Il6 and Cxcl10 (Fig. 4g); MeCP2 loss did not alter responses to the cGAS-independent agonist poly(I:C) (Fig. 4h), establishing specificity for the dsDNA-cGAS axis.
- **Absence of MeCP2 enforces an antiviral state.** HSV-1 (dsDNA genome; KOS64 and McKrae clones) induced cytosolic MeCP2, whereas VSV (ssRNA genome) did not (Fig. 5b-d; Supplementary Fig. 5b). MEF^gMecp2 showed increased Ifnβ, Cxcl10, Oasl1, Oas3, Il6 after HSV-1 infection, reduced HSV-1 plaque formation, and conditioned media that less efficiently transmitted infection — but VSV replication was unaffected (Fig. 5e-h).
- **A type I IFN / antiviral signature is present in MeCP2-deficient models and RTT patients.** RNA-seq of RAW264.7^gMecp2 enriched for stress/immune-response GSEA terms; Mecp2^-/y mouse liver showed increased Oas1b, Oas2, Oasl2, Oas3, Ccl5, Slfn5, Mx2, Isg15 (mean-centroid significant); meta-analysis of MeCP2-silenced mouse hippocampus and RTT patient samples (varying severity) showed IFNα/IFNγ/STAT5 and innate-immunity DEGs (Fig. 5i-m).
- **MeCP2 nuclear export de-represses endogenous retroelements, especially LINE-1.** Telescope retrotranscriptome analysis of dsDNA-stimulated WT-RAW264.7 found 726 unique upregulated retroelement transcripts, with significant increases in class I retroelements (LTR/ERV, non-LTR LINEs and SINEs) and specifically LINE-1 (Fig. 6a-c); WT-MEF showed the same LINE upregulation (Fig. 6d).
- **MeCP2 deficiency itself raises LINE-1, additively with dsDNA.** In RAW264.7, dsDNA increased LINE-1 transcripts ~3.1-fold; MeCP2 deficiency alone raised LINE-1 ~2.3-fold, marginally further increased (~1.1-fold) by dsDNA — implicating MeCP2 displacement in dsDNA-induced LINE-1 activation (Fig. 6e).
- **De-repressed LINE-1 produces ORF1p and immunogenic cytosolic LINE-1 DNA.** dsDNA increased cytosolic ORF1p (an essential LINE-1 retrotransposition component) in MEF and RAW264.7, and HSV-1 also raised ORF1p (Fig. 6f,g). Nanopore sequencing of cytosolic DNA showed a 10-fold increase in LINE-1 reads after dsDNA stimulation (Fig. 6h).
- **LINE-1 reverse-transcriptase activity sustains persistent IFN responses.** Tenofovir had only mild effect at 6 h but dampened Cxcl10, Isg15, Oas2 and Oasl1 induction at 24 h post-dsDNA, indicating LINE-1-derived DNA maintains persistence of type I IFN signaling (Fig. 6i), nominating RT inhibitors to resolve chronic inflammation in RTT.

## Methods

- **DNA-binding biochemistry:** in vitro and in-cell biotinylated nucleic-acid pulldowns (streptavidin/Dynabeads M280) with 80-bp synthetic ssDNA, dsDNA (two sequences), and dsRNA probes; whole-cell, cytosolic and nuclear (Dignam/S100) extracts; Western blot readout of MeCP2, cGas, Sting and phospho-Sting/Tbk1/Irf3/Stat1.
- **Co-IP / interaction:** endogenous MeCP2 immunoprecipitation; FLAG-MeCP2 (pOZ, from Addgene #110186) IP time-course; EGFP-cGAS and EGFP-tagged constructs for colocalization (JACoP/ImageJ Pearson's).
- **Genetic perturbation:** CRISPR-Cas9 (LentiCRISPRv2puro) Mecp2-targeting gRNAs (two distinct) vs non-targeting control in MEF and RAW264.7; cGas^-/-, Sting^-/- and Trex1^-/- MEF; MeCP2 overexpression rescue.
- **Pathway readouts:** intracellular 2'3'-cGAMP ELISA (Cayman); RT-qPCR of Ifnβ/Il6/Cxcl10/Ccl5/Isg15/Oas1/Oas2/Oas3/Oasl1/Mx2/Slfn5; Proteome Profiler Mouse Chemokine XL array.
- **Pharmacology:** Leptomycin B (nuclear export, CRM1); H-151 (STING inhibitor); Tenofovir (reverse-transcriptase / LINE-1 inhibitor).
- **Virology:** HSV-1 KOS64-GFP and engineered McKrae-EGFP (dsDNA virus) and VSV-GFP (ssRNA virus); plaque assays, conditioned-media transfer, immunofluorescence of viral and MeCP2 staining.
- **Transcriptomics:** RNA-seq (HISAT2/DESeq2; mm9) plus Telescope retrotranscriptome analysis of endogenous retroelements; nanopore (MinION) sequencing of cytosolic DNA mapped with Telescope to quantify LINE-1.
- **In vivo / patient data:** Mecp2-deficient (B6.129P2(c)Mecp2^tm1-1Bird) male mouse liver gene expression; meta-analysis of MeCP2-silenced hippocampus (GSE107004) and RTT patient samples (GSE198856); ORF1p immunofluorescence.

## Open Questions

- Does cytosolic MeCP2 form, or partition into, any membraneless condensate when bound to dsDNA, or does it act as a diffuse competitor of cGAS?
- cGAS and MeCP2 both bind pericentromeric/retroelement-rich sequences; do they bind overlapping genomic regions, and how does this shape detection of incoming viral dsDNA (e.g., HSV-1)?
- Is MeCP2 mislocalization a driver of the chronic IFN responses seen in Trex1 deficiency / Aicardi-Goutières syndrome, and would RT inhibitors act partly through MeCP2-dependent LINE-1 control?
- Can RNA viruses (e.g., VSV) exploit or co-opt MeCP2 during their life cycle, explaining why the MeCP2-loss antiviral state restricts HSV-1 but not VSV?
- Could chronic cGas-Sting activation downstream of MeCP2 loss explain the reported hepatic PUFA/metabolic dysfunction in MeCP2-deficient mice, and would STING inhibition benefit RTT patients?
