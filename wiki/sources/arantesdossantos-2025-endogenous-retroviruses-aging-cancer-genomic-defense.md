# Endogenous retroviruses in aging and cancer: from genomic defense to oncogenic activation

_Mobile DNA, 2025._

## Summary

This review (Mobile DNA 2025, 16:45) examines the paradoxical "double-edged" relationship between endogenous retroviruses (ERVs/HERVs) and cancer, framed through aging and evolution. ERVs are LTR retrotransposons derived from ancient germline retroviral infections, constituting roughly 8% of the human genome (transposable elements as a whole ~50%); most loci are truncated solo LTRs that have lost the ability to make infectious particles. Normally silenced by DNA methylation and repressive histone marks (e.g., H3K9me3), ERVs are exapted for legitimate host functions — alternative promoters/enhancers, regulatory ncRNAs, chimeric transcripts, and co-opted proteins (syncytins in placentation, immune-gene regulation, embryonic/CNS roles). The authors' central thesis is that age- or stress-associated derepression of ERVs acts as an evolutionarily conserved genomic-surveillance ("sentinel") system: ERV-derived dsRNA and viral-like proteins trigger innate-immune dsRNA sensors and interferon pathways, driving senescence and immunosurveillance that can eliminate pre-malignant cells. They advance the novel hypothesis that ERV fixation in mammalian genomes was selected partly as an anti-tumorigenic defense. Conversely, established tumors can hijack ERV expression to drive chronic inflammation, SASP-like senescence, stemness, and onco-exaptation of oncogenes, facilitating progression. Finally, the review discusses exploiting ERV "viral mimicry" therapeutically — DNMT inhibitors and HDAC inhibitors reactivate ERVs to induce interferon responses, neoantigens, and checkpoint-inhibitor sensitivity — and links HERV biology to immunosenescence and inflammaging. This is a narrative review; all mechanistic claims are attributed to cited primary studies, and no new experiments are presented. There is **no P-body content**; relevance to the P-body SEG is limited to shared retrotransposon-defense and viral-mimicry / interferon (immune-ISG) axes.

## Key Points

- ERVs/HERVs are LTR retrotransposons (Class I gamma/epsilon-, Class II beta-, Class III spuma-related) from ancient germline infections; ~8% of the human genome, mostly solo LTRs and replication-incompetent.
- Normally silenced epigenetically (DNA methylation, H3K9me3); age-associated epigenetic dysregulation progressively reactivates them, linking ERVs to senescence, the DNA-damage response, and age-related disease including cancer.
- Proposed anti-oncogenic "sentinel" role: TP53 activation induces ERV expression (via inhibition of LSD1 and DNMT1) while suppressing other retrotransposons; resulting dsRNA triggers dsRNA sensors and interferon pathways for an anti-tumoral immune response (cited from a primary study).
- Naked mole rats (cancer-resistant) show low DNMT1 and similar antiviral-pathway activation; cancer-resistant species preserve ERV content while reducing LINE/SINE load — argued as evidence for an ERV protective role.
- Oncogenic side: onco-exaptation (e.g., THE1B LTR driving aberrant CSF1R in lymphoma; HERV-K LTR translocation upregulating ETV1 in prostate cancer), enhancer activity in AML, HERV-K–driven cancer stemness (CD133/Oct4) in melanoma, and HERV-K Env in pancreatic cancer proliferation/neoantigens.
- Cancer can hijack ERVs to promote chronic inflammation and SASP-like senescence (pro-tumoral); ERV impact is strongly context- and stage-dependent and can act as a biomarker.
- Therapeutic "viral mimicry": DNMT inhibitors (azacitidine, decitabine) ± HDAC inhibitors induce ERV-derived dsRNA sensed by the MDA5–MAVS pathway, triggering interferon, antigen presentation, cancer-testis antigens, and checkpoint molecules (PD-L1, CTLA-4), sensitizing tumors to immune-checkpoint blockade.
- HERV expression varies during aging and intersects with immunosenescence and inflammaging in a possibly element-specific manner (some HERVs anti-, some pro-immunosenescence).

## Scope

Covers: ERV/HERV genomic structure, classification, and epigenetic regulation; physiological exaptation (gene regulation, placentation via syncytins, embryonic/CNS/immune roles); ERVs in cancer (onco-exaptation, stemness, enhancer/promoter activity, recombination); a proposed evolutionarily conserved anti-oncogenic sentinel function tied to dsRNA sensing, interferon, senescence, and immunosurveillance; cancer hijacking of ERVs (inflammation, SASP); and therapeutic exploitation via DNMTi/HDACi-induced viral mimicry and immunotherapy, plus immunosenescence/inflammaging. As a narrative review it presents no new primary data and attributes every mechanistic statement to cited studies. It does **not** address P-bodies, mRNA decapping/decay granules, or P-body-resident proteins; intersection with the P-body corpus is only at the conceptual level of retrotransposon control and viral-mimicry/interferon signaling.

## Open Questions

- What are the specific molecular mechanisms by which preserved ERV content (vs. reduced LINE/SINE load) confers tumor suppression in cancer-resistant species such as naked mole rats and bats?
- Is the proposed ERV "sentinel" / genomic-surveillance function — dsRNA sensing and interferon induction on stress-triggered ERV derepression — mechanistically distinct from, or convergent with, P-body/cytoplasmic RNA-granule control of retroelement RNAs?
- What determines whether ERV reactivation in a given cell yields anti-tumor surveillance versus pro-tumor inflammation/SASP — cell state, driver mutations, or stochastic context?
- What is the role of HERVs in immunosenescence and inflammaging, and how does this modulate immunotherapy efficacy, especially in older patients?
- Which HERV families/loci have sufficient tumor restriction and normal-tissue absence to be safe immunotherapy targets, and how should off-tumor risk be de-risked?
