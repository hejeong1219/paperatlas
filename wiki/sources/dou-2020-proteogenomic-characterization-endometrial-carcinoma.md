# Proteogenomic Characterization of Endometrial Carcinoma

_Cell, 2020._

DOI: [10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.026)

## Summary

This CPTAC study performs proteogenomic characterization of 95 prospectively collected endometrial carcinomas (83 endometrioid, 12 serous) with 49 normal samples, integrating DNA/RNA layers with deep TMT-based proteome plus phosphoproteome and an extensive acetylome survey. The paper reports subtype-linked oncogenic pathway activity (e.g., p53 and Wnt/β-catenin), protein/PTM consequences of somatic drivers, and an immune landscape view that includes immunogenic alterations and antigen presentation defects (notably in MSI tumors).

## Key Points

- Multi-omic design includes whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing, transcriptome (including miRNA), DNA methylation, and TMT-based proteome + phosphoproteome + acetylome layers.
- The cohort is framed in the TCGA-style genomic subtypes (POLE, MSI, CNV-low, CNV-high) and connects driver events to protein/phosphoprotein states (cis/trans effects).
- The study highlights PTM-level regulatory mechanisms (phosphorylation/acetylation) that are not fully explained by RNA-level signals.
- Immune landscape analysis includes neoantigen-related features and points to antigen processing/presentation deficiencies as a potential explanation for limited checkpoint benefit in some MSI tumors.
