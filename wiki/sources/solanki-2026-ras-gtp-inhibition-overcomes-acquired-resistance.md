# RAS-GTP Inhibition Overcomes Acquired Resistance to KRASG12C Inhibitors Mediated by Oncogenic and Wild-Type RAS Activation in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer.

Solanki et al. use KRASG12C inhibitor-resistant NSCLC cell-line, xenograft, and PDX models to show that acquired resistance can split into genomic RAS reactivation, non-genomic RTK/RAS-GTP reactivation, and EMT-associated RAS-independent cell-cycle/DDR dependency states.

## Key Points

- **Claim-audit classification:** acquired-resistance evidence, but primarily **preclinical/model-system**, not a direct longitudinal patient cohort. The models were generated by chronic drug exposure or on-treatment relapse under sotorasib, adagrasib, or RMC-4998 pressure.
- **Data layers:** WES, RNA-seq, TMT-based global phosphoproteomics/proteomics, pY/global phosphosite analysis, KSEA, RAS-GTP pulldown, LC-MRM RAS isoform quantification, phospho-RTK arrays, CRISPR kinome screen, small-molecule testing, xenograft/PDX treatment validation.
- **Three resistance-state classes emerged:** (1) additional RAS alterations such as KRASG12C amplification or acquired NRASG13R; (2) RTK upregulation/activation with persistent wild-type RAS-GTP signaling despite no clear WES driver; (3) EMT/lineage plasticity with reduced RAS dependence and acquired cell-cycle/DDR vulnerabilities.
- **Genome-unexplained resistance is central:** in several resistant models, WES did not reveal RAS/MAPK-axis alterations predicted to confer resistance. RNA-seq and phosphoproteomics then implicated RTK signaling, MAPK/PI3K pathway rebound, and RAS-GTP persistence.
- **Phosphoproteomics is not decorative:** KSEA and manually curated phosphosite networks nominated ERBB-family, KDR/ephrin receptors, Src-family, ABL1, PRKCD, ITK, MAPK3/ERK1, CDK5, and cell-cycle kinases depending on the resistant state.
- **Functional validation followed the omics:** RMC-7977, a RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor, suppressed ERK signaling and induced regressions in RTK/RAS-dependent resistant models; RMC-4998 + RMC-7977 was effective in KRASG12C amplification and NRASG13R settings. EMT/RAS-low models instead showed sensitivity to cell-cycle/DDR stressors such as Aurora/PLK/CDK-axis inhibitors and TAK-243.
- **Use for this wiki project:** this paper strongly supports the working hypothesis that the genome-unexplained fraction of acquired resistance can be organized by functional signaling layers. It should not be used as patient-level proof of longitudinal proteogenomic resistance evolution, but it is a high-value mechanistic template.

## Claim Audit

| Question | Audit result |
| --- | --- |
| Is this primary or acquired resistance? | Acquired resistance in generated models: resistant cell lines, treatment-relapsed xenografts, and treatment-relapsed PDX models. |
| Does it have same-patient clinical pre/post data? | No. It parallels clinical mechanisms but is not a direct paired patient cohort. |
| Does WES explain all resistance states? | No. Some models had KRASG12C amplification or NRASG13R, but multiple resistant models lacked discernible WES drivers. |
| What layer explains WES-negative resistance? | RTK RNA/protein activation, phosphoproteomic/KSEA kinase signals, RAS-GTP pulldown/LC-MRM, and EMT/cell-cycle/DDR networks. |
| What claim is safe? | Functional omics can stratify acquired resistance models into RAS-dependent and RAS-independent resistant states. |
| What claim would overreach? | That patient-acquired resistance can be clinically classified by this signature without paired patient validation. |

## Evidence Details

### Resistance Models and WES Findings

The study built eleven resistant models across in vitro-selected cell lines, sotorasib-relapsed xenograft-derived models, and adagrasib- or RMC-4998-relapsed LUN156 NSCLC PDX models.

WES identified clear genomic RAS reactivation in only part of the model set:

- NCI-H358-MX1 acquired **NRASG13R** under sotorasib + SHP2 inhibitor pressure.
- MR2 showed **KRASG12C amplification**, with nearly 20 KRAS copies by amplicon-level copy-number analysis and increased KRAS-GTP signaling.
- Several other models had no secondary RAS/MAPK-axis alteration predicted to explain resistance, motivating non-genomic resistance analysis.

This directly matches the LLM-wiki claim-audit gap: if WES is negative, the resistance question moves to functional signaling layers.

### RTK / RAS-GTP Resistant State

In LU65-derived sotorasib-R and RMC-4998-R models, RNA-seq showed elevated RTK programs including ERBB2, ERBB3, IGF1R, DDR1, EGFR, MET, AXL, and PDGFRB depending on model.

TMT phosphoproteomics plus KSEA inferred activation of RTKs and non-RTKs, including ERBB-family kinases, KDR, ephrin receptors, Src-family kinases, ABL1, PRKCD, and ITK. Manual phosphosite curation highlighted EGFR/HER2/IGF1R and downstream MAPK and PI3K-AKT signaling. EGFR Y1068 phosphorylation, ERK phosphorylation, and AKT phosphorylation were validated by western blot.

RAS-GTP pulldown plus LC-MRM confirmed persistent active wild-type RAS isoforms in resistant LU65 cells. RMC-7977 suppressed ERK signaling and produced strong anti-tumor activity in resistant xenografts, indicating that some genome-unexplained resistance remains RAS-GTP dependent.

### PDX RTK / MAPK Resistant State

Adagrasib-R and RMC-4998-R LUN156 PDX models showed elevated RTK transcript programs. Phospho-RTK arrays confirmed increased MET and EGFR phosphorylation. Amivantamab plus RMC-4998 caused tumor regression in RMC-4998-R LUN156, supporting EGFR/MET-driven resistance.

KSEA in resistant PDX tumors implicated kinases such as MAPK3/ERK1, MAP2K4, PRKCA, YES1, and CDK5. Resistant tumors showed restored pERK and DUSP6 after earlier suppression at end-of-treatment time points. RMC-7977 monotherapy or RMC-4998 + RMC-7977 induced complete regression in LUN156 resistant xenograft models, although regrowth after treatment cessation indicated residual disease risk.

### EMT / RAS-Independent Resistant State

Sotorasib-R NCI-H358 cells and several MR models acquired EMT-like features: increased N-cadherin/vimentin and decreased E-cadherin. These models showed reduced RAS-GTP activity and lower dependence on RAS pathway inhibition.

RNA-seq and phosphoproteomics instead nominated EMT, WNT/NOTCH, cell-cycle, and DNA-damage response programs. KSEA highlighted CDK1, AURKA, PLK4, AURKB, TTK, and NEK2; kinome CRISPR screening identified dependencies including PLK1, ATR, CHEK1, and CDK1. A curated phosphoproteomic network centered on TP53BP1 and included RIF1, NBN, PRKDC, NSD2, UIMC1, PARP1, and H2AX.

The model was preferentially sensitive to cell-cycle/DDR stressors including Aurora kinase inhibitors, etoposide, and TAK-243. This supports a second functional lesson: some acquired resistance states are no longer best treated as RAS-pathway reactivation but as lineage/plasticity-associated dependency switching.

## Cancer Multiomics Project Relevance

- **For acquired-resistance framing:** Solanki 2026 is a strong mechanistic example of why acquired resistance should be treated as multiple states rather than a single resistant label.
- **For WGS-limitation framing:** it directly shows WES-positive and WES-negative resistance classes inside the same resistance project.
- **For phosphoproteomics framing:** KSEA, phosphosite network curation, phospho-RTK arrays, and RAS-GTP LC-MRM show how functional signaling layers can classify WES-negative resistant states.
- **For the current refractory gastric cancer question:** it supports the idea that kinase-driven immune-quiet or signaling-driven resistant states should be framed as functional resistance states unless paired clinical timing proves acquired evolution.

## Open Questions

- Which of the three resistance-state classes transfers to patient biopsies after KRASG12C inhibitor failure?
- Can paired patient pre/post phosphoproteomics detect the same RTK/RAS-GTP or EMT/cell-cycle/DDR state transitions?
- Does immune remodeling accompany these RAS-dependent versus RAS-independent resistant states in patient tumors?
- How should RAS-GTP activity be approximated in bulk patient proteogenomic datasets where pulldown/LC-MRM is unavailable?
